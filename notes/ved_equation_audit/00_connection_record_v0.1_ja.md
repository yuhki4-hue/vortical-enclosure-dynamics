## 5. What finite stress tests supplied

### T1 — observable independence

[T1](../../generative_law_methodology/stress_tests/observable_independence_finite_model_v0.1_ja.md)
separated explicit definability, raw fiber multiplicity, observational quotient, distinguishable
variation, and mechanism.

\[
\text{new observable}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{independent generation}.
\]

The diagnostic remained relative to the chosen projection, observables, and source completeness.

### T2 — graph-growth dual encoding

[T2](../../generative_law_methodology/stress_tests/graph_growth_dual_encoding_v0.1_ja.md)
encoded bounded graph growth both as fixed-state rewriting and changing-carrier records. Reachable
states, selected predicates, and transitions were preserved under the fixed translation; stage-local
language and future reference differed conditionally with the admissibility policy.

### T3 — construction and internal order

[T3](../../generative_law_methodology/stress_tests/construction_vs_internal_order_v0.1_ja.md)
separated construction order, internal partial order, timestamps, duration, and physical time.
Different linear extensions could yield the same partial order; no physical-time conclusion followed.

### T4 — relation-to-metric boundary

[T4](../../generative_law_methodology/stress_tests/relation_to_metric_boundary_v0.1_ja.md)
separated relation, weighting, shortest-path metric construction, and coarse-metric policy. It did not
model physical interpretation, so physical space remained **NOT ESTABLISHED** rather than disproved.

### T5 — rule space as data

[T5](../../generative_law_methodology/stress_tests/rule_space_as_data_v0.1_ja.md)
encoded several finite rule changes as state under a fixed interpreter. Stage-local availability,
type formation, interpreter boundaries, and open rule/meta hierarchies remained preload conditions or
**OPEN** questions; no universal reduction followed.

## 8. Preload correspondence

The later controls repeatedly required explicit records of what a fixed representation places in its
specification before a run.

- **Structural:** maximal carriers, candidate edges, state/type universes.
- **Semantic / language:** name registries, observable languages, stage-local well-formedness rules.
- **Operational:** rule repertoires, rule-code syntax, interpreters, admissible meta-transitions.
- **Measurement / interpretation:** weights, path aggregation, observational policies,
  coarse-graining maps and policies.

This provides a later audit correspondence to the earlier `transparent background` question, but:

\[
\text{transparent background}
\neq
\text{preload as an identical concept}.
\]

Moreover:

\[
\text{representation preload}
\neq
\text{physical ontology}
\neq
\text{system knowledge}
\neq
\text{predetermination}.
\]

`preload` remains an ordinary audit description of representation choices, not a new ontology or
technical theory.
