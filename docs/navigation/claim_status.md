# Claim Status and Scope

This document separates the different strengths of claim used across VED. It is
intended to prevent two opposite misreadings:

1. reading every VED statement as a completed physical derivation, and
2. reducing the framework to metaphor, analogy, or loose philosophical language.

VED is best read as an open closure-dynamics research program. It contains
foundational commitments, working formalisms, structural reinterpretations,
exploratory physical reconstructions, and application-layer accounts. These do
not all carry the same evidential or mathematical burden.

## Claim Hierarchy

| Level | Status | Typical Content | How to Read It |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Foundational stance | difference, non-closure, causal asymmetry | Minimal starting orientation for the framework. |
| 1 | Working formalism | `C_ij`, causal log, closure degree, generative sequence | A proposed formal grammar for modeling closure dynamics; not the only possible formalization. |
| 2 | Structural reinterpretation | time, space, observer, measurement, effective closure | A way to re-situate existing descriptive structures inside VED vocabulary. |
| 3 | Exploratory physical reconstruction | gravity, cosmology, gauge-like closure, Standard-Model-like structure | Speculative reconstruction paths; not completed quantitative derivations. |
| 4 | Application-layer account | life, consciousness, intelligence, conceptual topography, social stopping | Cross-scale use of the same vocabulary to compare closure, non-closure, residuals, and stopping operations. |

## What “Derivation” Means Here

Some VED documents use terms such as “derive,” “emerge,” or “generate.” These
terms should be read according to claim level.

- At Levels 0-1, they refer to the internal ordering of VED terms.
- At Level 2, they refer to structural re-situation rather than replacement of
  established theories.
- At Level 3, they mark candidate reconstruction paths and open derivation
  programs.
- At Level 4, they mark conditions under which a higher-layer regime may be
  described using closure/non-closure vocabulary.

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, VED does **not** claim a completed
quantitative derivation in the sense required to replace quantum field theory,
general relativity, the Standard Model, neuroscience, or cognitive science.

## Not Claimed

VED does not currently claim that:

- the Standard Model has been quantitatively derived in full;
- particle masses, coupling constants, mixing angles, or all representation
  data have been calculated from first principles;
- general relativity or quantum field theory have been replaced;
- qualia or consciousness have been solved as completed explanatory objects;
- intelligence has been reduced to a single internal mechanism;
- existing theories are obsolete or unnecessary.

## Positively Claimed

VED does claim that:

- non-closure can be treated as constitutive rather than merely defective;
- effective theories can be compared by how they close, where they leave
  residuals, and how those residuals shape later descriptions;
- causal-log, closure-degree, and horizon-like vocabulary can provide a common
  structural grammar across physical, biological, cognitive, consciousness,
  intelligence, and social layers;
- several existing boundary problems may be easier to compare when read as
  problems of closure, non-closure, residuals, and cross-scale registration.

## Use in Evaluation

When evaluating VED, first identify the claim level being made. A Level 3
physical reconstruction should not be evaluated as if it were already a completed
Level 1 formal derivation. Conversely, a Level 4 application should not be
accepted merely because it shares vocabulary with the foundational sequence.

The framework remains open. Its stronger claims require further formalization,
benchmarks, simulations, and comparison with established theories.
