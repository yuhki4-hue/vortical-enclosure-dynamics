# Lineage and Reference Anchors

This document gives readers a set of landing points in existing research
traditions. It is not a full bibliography and does not claim that VED is derived
from any single prior framework.

VED was developed in dialogue with several neighboring traditions, but it is not
presented as an extension of only one of them. The purpose of this document is to
make that dialogue visible without turning the main papers into citation-led
exposition.

## Why This Document Exists

Large cross-domain frameworks can look isolated when they do not show their
conceptual neighbors. VED deliberately keeps its main generative exposition
light, but readers still need orientation points.

These anchors serve four purposes:

1. show that VED is not ignoring existing literature;
2. prevent collapse into any one neighboring theory;
3. clarify which problems VED shares with existing traditions;
4. make future citation expansion easier without weakening the main flow of the
   papers.

## How to Use These Anchors

These are not dependency claims. They are routing markers.

- **Neighbor** means a nearby tradition, not an identity claim.
- **Constraint** means an existing theory or result that VED should remain
  compatible with or explain its relation to.
- **Contrast** means a theory that asks related questions but closes the problem
  differently.
- **Citation candidate** means a work or author that may be useful when expanding
  formal references in future versions.

## Physical and Mathematical Anchors

| Area | Why It Matters for VED | Typical Anchors |
|---|---|---|
| General relativity | Gravity, geometry, horizon structure, spacetime dynamics | Einstein; Misner, Thorne & Wheeler; Wald |
| Quantum field theory | Field structure, renormalization, divergence, effective description | Weinberg; Peskin & Schroeder; Wilson; Kogut |
| Renormalization / effective theories | Scale-dependent closure and residual structure | Wilson; Weinberg; modern EFT literature |
| Quantum foundations | Measurement, observer-dependence, non-classical registration | Bohr; von Neumann; Bell; decoherence literature; Rovelli |
| Dynamical systems | Flow, attractors, stability, bifurcation-like structure | Strogatz; Guckenheimer & Holmes |
| Non-equilibrium and dissipative structures | Persistence through flow and maintained organization | Prigogine; Nicolis; Haken |
| Information geometry | Metric structure induced by informational relations | Amari |

VED should not be read as replacing these traditions. It asks how their
successful local closures and remaining boundary problems might be compared
through closure, non-closure, residuals, and causal registration.

## Biological and Cognitive Anchors

| Area | Why It Matters for VED | Typical Anchors |
|---|---|---|
| Autopoiesis | Living systems as self-maintaining organization | Maturana & Varela |
| Enactivism | Cognition as organism-environment coupling | Varela, Thompson & Rosch; Noe; Di Paolo |
| Ecological psychology | Perception and action in environment-relative terms | Gibson |
| Embodied cognition | Cognitive processes constrained by body and action | Clark; Thompson |
| Extended mind / distributed cognition | Intelligence beyond individual internal representation | Clark & Chalmers; Hutchins |
| Predictive processing | Prediction, update, error, and hierarchical dynamics | Rao & Ballard; Clark; Hohwy |
| Free Energy Principle / active inference | Stability, minimization, viable regimes, inference-action coupling | Friston |

VED overlaps most strongly with these areas in its treatment of life,
intelligence, and consciousness as ongoing regimes rather than completed objects.
The difference is that VED places these regimes inside a broader vocabulary of
closure dynamics rather than beginning from cognition or life alone.

## Consciousness-Theory Anchors

| Area | Why It Matters for VED | Typical Anchors |
|---|---|---|
| Hard problem / subjective character | Why functional or structural accounts face explanatory pressure | Nagel; Chalmers |
| Global Workspace-type theories | Availability, broadcast, reportability, access | Baars; Dehaene; Naccache |
| Integrated Information Theory | Integration, differentiation, system-level structure | Tononi; Oizumi; Koch |
| Sensorimotor / enactive accounts | Experience as active embodied coupling | O'Regan & Noe; Varela; Thompson |
| Higher-order / self-model accounts | Reflexivity, self-reference, representation of mental states | Rosenthal; Metzinger |

VED does not claim to have solved qualia. Its consciousness layer should be read
as an attempt to describe dynamic conditions under which subjective persistence,
attention, self-reference, and reportable experience may appear as temporal
non-closure and cross-scale log referencing.

## AI, Intelligence, and Social-Stopping Anchors

| Area | Why It Matters for VED | Typical Anchors |
|---|---|---|
| Artificial intelligence | Externalized inference, model interaction, token-mediated conceptual flow | Turing; Newell & Simon; transformer literature |
| Distributed cognition | Intelligence as socially and environmentally scaffolded | Hutchins |
| Cultural evolution | Shared constraints and inherited stopping operations | Boyd & Richerson; Henrich |
| Pragmatism | Meaning and truth as enacted, tested, and revised in practice | Peirce; James; Dewey |
| Sociology of knowledge | Knowledge as historically stabilized social practice | Kuhn; Bourdieu; Latour |

The intelligence and social layers of VED are closest to the idea that stopping,
judgment, inference, and understanding are not sealed inside an individual mind.
They emerge through logs, tools, institutions, environments, and shared
constraint systems.

## Comparison Principle

For each neighboring theory, VED should eventually answer four questions:

1. What problem does the neighboring theory close well?
2. What residual or boundary does it leave open?
3. What does VED preserve from that theory?
4. What does VED re-situate using closure/non-closure vocabulary?

This keeps citation from becoming mere authority signaling. The role of
reference anchors is to make VED easier to locate, compare, criticize, and
improve.
