# GWT Comparison

This page is a navigation aid for readers who approach VED from Global
Workspace-type theories (GWT). It does not claim that VED is a Global Workspace
theory, nor that GWT is reducible to VED.

## Why This Comparison Matters

GWT-oriented readers are likely to notice themes that also appear in the
consciousness paper:

- conscious states are not merely local events,
- information can become available to multiple processing systems,
- broad availability and reportability matter,
- temporal referencing changes how current processing becomes usable.

The main repository basis for this comparison is the consciousness paper's
explicit discussion of Global Workspace-type models.

## Agreement

VED / IFGT and GWT can be read as sharing several broad concerns.

| Shared Orientation | VED / IFGT Reading |
|---|---|
| Consciousness is not merely local | Consciousness is treated as a relation among multiple processing layers. |
| Availability matters | Broad availability may be part of the result or conditions of a conscious state. |
| Reportability matters | Log referencing and reportability are treated as important pathways. |
| Isolated content is insufficient | The key issue is how processing becomes referenceable across time and systems. |

## Difference

The explanatory focus is different.

| GWT | VED / IFGT |
|---|---|
| Often centers global broadcasting or availability. | Centers temporal transformation into log-referenceable structure. |
| Spatial breadth of availability can be central. | Temporal transformation is more central than spatial breadth. |
| Consciousness may be defined by global access. | Global access may be a condition or result, but is not consciousness itself. |

The consciousness paper explicitly states that information becoming broadly
available may be part of the result or conditions of a conscious state, but is
not consciousness itself.

## Possible Relation

Within this repository, GWT may be interpreted as a neighboring account of one
important aspect of conscious availability. A cautious formulation is:

> GWT can be read as describing how information becomes broadly usable across
> processing systems, while VED / IFGT asks when processing becomes temporally
> sedimented and referenceable as a log within a non-closure regime.

This possible relation does not imply identity between the frameworks.

## Interpretation Boundaries

Do not read this comparison as claiming that:

- VED proves GWT,
- GWT is reducible to VED,
- VED replaces GWT,
- broadcasting itself is consciousness in the VED account,
- global availability is irrelevant to VED,
- the repository already contains a complete formal translation between GWT and
  VED.
