# Methodology

This note collects the short methodological summary that previously appeared in the top-level README and places it alongside the longer methodological note.

## Methodological Position

VED does not begin from conservation laws.

Conservation emerges as an effective property of fixed-point regimes:

$$\nabla\cdot J \approx 0, \qquad \sigma \approx 0$$

The framework intentionally avoids assuming a Lagrangian or action principle at the foundational level. Such structures may emerge at coarse-grained scales, but are not taken as primitive.

## Why the Framework Does Not Begin from Conservation Law

VED does not begin from conservation law because conservation already presupposes stable quantities, admissible transformations, and a formal background in which equivalence across change can be stated. Those are powerful structures, but they are not minimal structures.

The methodological choice here is to start earlier. If the aim is to ask how ordered physical structure might arise at all, then one should avoid taking for granted the invariants that are usually formulated only after a system has already acquired a sufficiently stable description. In VED, conservation is therefore treated as something that may emerge within persistent closure regimes, rather than something imposed as the first principle.

This is not a rejection of conservation law. It is a claim about logical order. Stable recurrence, closure integrity, and coarse-grained persistence may be the conditions under which conservation statements become meaningful. A theory that starts from difference and closure should therefore permit conservation to appear where warranted, without requiring it at the base layer.

## Why an Action-First or Lagrangian-First Base Is Not Assumed

The same reasoning applies to action-first formalism. Lagrangians and actions are extraordinarily effective once one has already identified generalized coordinates, symmetry assumptions, and a space of admissible histories. But these tools encode substantial structure from the outset.

VED intentionally withholds that structure at the base level. The project is not to deny the usefulness of variational methods, but to avoid assuming them before the entities, distances, and histories over which a variational principle would range have themselves been generated. In that sense, the framework treats action-based description as potentially emergent, derivative, or approximate rather than primitive.

This methodological choice protects the theory from premature closure. If geometry, duration, and persistent objecthood are themselves products of deeper closure processes, then an action-first formalism may be exact only after the relevant level of structure has stabilized.

## Incompleteness as Participation Space

The framework is intentionally incomplete in public form. This incompleteness should not be read as rhetorical evasion; it is a methodological condition of the project at its current stage. A structural theory that is still moving from generative concepts to quantitative consequence cannot honestly present open derivational gaps as if they were already solved.

For that reason, VED treats incompleteness as a participation space.

This means:

- unresolved derivations are identified rather than hidden
- conceptual transitions are exposed to criticism
- quantitative targets are stated as research tasks
- later formal closure is not simulated before it is earned

Participation space does not mean that anything goes. The framework still demands internal coherence, explicit assumptions, and resistance to contradiction. What remains open is not the standard of rigor, but the domain in which rigor must still be carried out.

## Research Posture

The research posture of VED is therefore constructive but non-defensive. It does not claim finality. It claims a proposed structural order and asks whether that order can be formalized, compared, criticized, and eventually made predictive. Beginning below conservation law and below action-first formalism is part of that wager. It is an attempt to keep the base layer as lean as possible so that later structure, if valid, appears as a consequence rather than an assumption.
