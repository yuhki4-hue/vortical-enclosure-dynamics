# Three Claims Worth Examining

This note preserves the stronger summary claims that previously appeared in the top-level README.

## 1. Conservation laws are not fundamental.

The base equation is $\partial_t\rho + \nabla\cdot J = \sigma$ with $\sigma > 0$.  
Conservation ($\sigma = 0$) is an effective law valid only in the classical fixed-point phase.  
Time itself is generated by $\sigma > 0$ — the Lagrangian principle $\delta S = 0$ presupposes what VED tries to derive.

## 2. Newton's constant $G$ is not a primitive constant.

$$G \sim \frac{\eta_\phi , \epsilon}{4\pi , \kappa_B^2} , F(\lambda, \beta, \ldots)$$

where $\kappa_B$ is the complete-closure prohibition barrier and $\epsilon$ is the closure-to-distance coupling.  
The universality of $G$ across particle species is a dynamic convergence in the classical phase, not an axiom.

## 3. Black hole singularities do not exist.

The interior is a phase, not a geometric extension. As $r \to 0$:

$$B(L) = -\kappa_B\log!\left(1-\frac{L}{L_{\max}}\right) \to \infty$$

Complete closure is prohibited by the same axiom that generates the world.  
The center is an asymptotic limit, not a divergence.
