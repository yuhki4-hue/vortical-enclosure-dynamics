\section{Introduction}

Many biological phenomena are observed as development, morphogenesis, differentiation, and evolution. These phenomena are often described using language such as design, program, or genetic instruction. Such language can be useful at an intuitive level, but its relation to the underlying physical and chemical processes is not always explicit.

DNA sequences constrain molecular production and reaction pathways. They do not directly specify a completed structure, purpose, or intention. The fact that cells with the same DNA can differentiate into distinct states illustrates this point. DNA is therefore represented here abstractly through a parameter set \(\theta\) that constrains the dynamics of the system; a concrete genotype-to-parameter map and parameter provenance are not supplied.

This paper describes biological systems as processes of state change and sustained structure. We use three source-local formal quantities: information density \(\I\), information flow \(\J\), and induced potential \(\PhiI\). They provide a common model description of development and morphogenesis, while their operational identification with biological observables remains separate.

The central feature of the framework is that it does not require design or purpose as explanatory primitives. Form and function are described as possible outcomes of state change under an attractor-based interpretation; convergence and stability in a concrete biological system are not established by the generic relations alone.

The aim is to describe existing phenomena in a consistent form. We combine a minimal set of variables, a continuity equation, and an attractor-based interpretation of stability.

Section 2 gives the intuitive picture. Section 3 defines the formal mapping. Section 4 introduces the core equations. Sections 5 and following apply the framework to biological phenomena, simulation, and scope.

This paper builds on the Information-Field-Geometry-Theory (IFGT), which formulates the motion of systems toward closure and the persistence of quasi-closure structures. In IFGT, the degree of closure \(C\) characterizes how far a system has progressed toward a stable configuration. In biological systems, the field \(\I\) should not be identified directly with the IFGT quantity \(I=f(1-C)\). It is a source-local, coarse-grained constraint-density field used to describe local structural constraint, differentiation, and retained bias within a particular observational window; no operational biological measurement map is supplied here. The relation is therefore a proposed correspondence between descriptive layers, not an identity, derivation, or typed translation between variables. The sustained non-equilibrium structure of living systems may likewise be interpreted, in IFGT terms, through quasi-closure. This proposed structural reading does not establish a biological mechanism or complete any upstream VED/IFGT bridge.
