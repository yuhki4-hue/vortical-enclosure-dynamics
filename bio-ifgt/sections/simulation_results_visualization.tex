\section{Simulation Results and Visualization}

This section reports a numerical run of the minimal developmental model introduced in the previous section. The aim is to show how structure appears over time in the toy system. The result is not intended as a reproduction of a specific biological process.

\subsection{Initial State}

At the initial time, the fields are nearly uniform and no organized structure is present. The morphogen-like model field \(M\) has only a localized source term. The model constraint-density field \(\I\) is low and has no meaningful spatial bias apart from a small perturbation. The neural-like commitment field \(N\) and the eye-like commitment field \(E\) are initially uncommitted model states.

In this state, no completed output array is supplied. The source profile and positional window nevertheless preload coarse spatial information used by the later updates.

\subsection{Time Evolution}

As the system evolves, structure appears in the following order:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the morphogen field diffuses and forms a smooth gradient,
  \item the model constraint-density field \(\I\) accumulates and develops spatial bias,
  \item thresholding produces a central neural-plate-like region in \(N\),
  \item a globally normalized input derived from \(|\nabla \I|\) updates the accumulated boundary field \(B\),
  \item the overlap of neural commitment, boundary stabilization, and a positional window produces localized eye-like commitment in \(E\).
\end{enumerate}
This list reports the staged presentation of the run. The actual loop is ordered: updated \(M\) feeds \(\I\), updated \(\I\) feeds both \(N\) and the normalized gradient input to \(B\), and updated \(N\) and \(B\) combine with the positional window in the \(E\) update. It does not contain a direct \(N\)-to-\(B\) input or a simultaneous-update assumption.
The manuscript describes the field process continuously, while the reported run is its source-specific finite-step implementation. No stage contains an encoded final drawing of the resulting structures.

\subsection{Final State}

At the reported 800-step horizon, the simulation shows a reproducible central neural-plate-like model region and two relatively bright eye-like lobes. The central \(E\) values are not zero, and the \(E>0.5\) mask forms one connected component in the audited default condition; this output is therefore not evidence for two independent eye structures. Localization is conditional on the explicit positional window, and disabling the eye pathway suppresses \(E\). This is a finite-horizon description: longer audit runs continue to evolve, and neither stationarity nor asymptotic stability is established.

Thus, the dynamics and supplied spatial constraints jointly shape the model's relative intensity pattern; the result is not geometry-free emergence or a biological eye-development mechanism.

\subsection{Summary of Observations}

The simulation illustrates the following points:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the neural-plate-like model region arises through thresholding of model constraint density,
  \item the eye-like field localizes through the combination of a gradient-sensitive accumulated state and explicit positional conditions,
  \item structure appears in stages,
  \item the completed output array is not specified in advance, although coarse source and window geometry is supplied.
\end{itemize}

The resulting structures are therefore interpreted as reproducible finite-horizon patterns under constrained dynamics. The run does not establish a stationary solution, invariant basin, or general perturbation return.

\subsection{Role of This Section}

This result is not a model of a specific biological phenomenon. The role of the simulation is to show finite spatial organization under the stated conditions in a field-based toy system. In this respect, the output is a qualitative structural analogue consistent with the Bio-IFGT interpretation developed in the preceding sections.

It does not demonstrate biological mechanism or empirical correspondence, but only model-local structural possibility. No external biological dataset or operational observable mapping is used.
