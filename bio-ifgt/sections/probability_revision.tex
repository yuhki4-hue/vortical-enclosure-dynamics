\section{Probability Revision}

This section revisits the formulation of the probability of the emergence of life. The aim is not to assign a numerical value, but to clarify what the probability is taken to measure.

\subsection{Conventional Formulation}

The emergence of life is often treated as the probability that a particular molecular structure appears by chance. In this view, many conditions must be satisfied simultaneously, and each may have low probability. The combined probability can therefore become very small.

This view is coherent if life is assumed to be a particular completed configuration. The present framework uses a different object of probability.

\subsection{The Issue}

Under the proposed terminology of Section 7, life is represented not by a fixed structure but by sustained motion in a bounded state-space region. This is a model target; its operational identification with biological life remains open.

The emergence of life is then interpreted as reaching this region and remaining within it, rather than hitting one specified structure.

\subsection{Probability as Region Entry}

For this conceptual reformulation, let the proposed life-attractor region be
\begin{equation}
  \Omega_{\mathrm{life}} \subset \Omega.
\end{equation}
The probability question is then reformulated as the probability of reaching \(\Omega_{\mathrm{life}}\). This is a region-entry target rather than an exact-point target; no sample space, measure, projection, or numerical probability is supplied.

\subsection{Structure of Trials}

In the schematic trial picture, molecular reactions and material cycles repeat as long as enabling conditions persist: non-persistent states disappear while persistent states remain. This motivates the region-entry reformulation; it is not a demonstrated biological maintenance mechanism or an independently measured selection process.

\subsection{Dimensional Reduction}

In a point-target view, many degrees of freedom must be specified simultaneously. The present framework proposes describing the relevant conditions through fewer effective variables that characterize entry into a candidate region. Without a projection and probability measure, this is a structural reformulation rather than an established quantitative dimensional reduction.

\subsection{Interpretation}

This is not a numerical reassessment of probability. It is a structural redefinition of the object being counted. Life is neither a miraculous hit nor an automatic necessity. It is a state that can appear when sustaining conditions exist.

In IFGT terms, this reformulation has a proposed structural interpretation: the Bio-IFGT life-region target may be compared with entry into and persistence within a quasi-closure regime. This is a correspondence of descriptive roles, not an identity, typed translation, or completed biological mechanism. The probability question may therefore be posed schematically as reaching and maintaining such a regime rather than assembling a fixed molecular configuration; no probability measure, empirical estimate, or IFGT/Bio bridge is supplied here.

\subsection{Connection to Intelligence}

This reformulation makes the next question more precise: after life is established, why do only some systems reach intelligence? Intelligence is not a simple continuation of life. It requires additional structural conditions. The next section treats intelligence as a second attractor.
