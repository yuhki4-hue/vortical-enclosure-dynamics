\section{Discussion}

This section summarizes the scope and limits of the framework. The aim is not to add new claims, but to clarify what the framework does and does not address.

\subsection{Scope of Description}

The paper describes biological systems using source-local information density \(\I\), information flow \(\J\), and induced potential \(\PhiI\). This is a minimal common language for treating development, morphogenesis, life maintenance, and the conditions for intelligence; it does not make the quantities operational biological observables or identical carriers across scales.

The framework extracts structure. It does not attempt detailed reproduction of each individual biological phenomenon. Its attractor, maintenance, and robustness language is conditional and interpretive; existence, stability, operational life criteria, and biological regeneration remain open.

\subsection{Relation to Existing Models}

The framework does not replace existing biological models. Reaction--diffusion systems, gene networks, and neural dynamics remain necessary at their own levels. The present formalism proposes a way to redescribe such models in terms of analogous structural roles: density, flow, induced bias, and stability. Reused vocabulary does not establish identical dynamics or a typed cross-scale map.

It does not require the assumption of a new mechanism, nor does it deny existing theories.

\subsection{Non-Closure and Sustained Dynamics}

The dynamics considered here do not generally converge to complete equilibrium or fixed closure. Flow continues, and structure is updated. In this sense, biological systems are interpreted as sustained non-closed structures.

This property is not developed as a separate theory in this paper. It appears as a descriptive feature of the dynamics considered.

This interpretation is proposed as structurally analogous to the quasi-closure vocabulary formalized in IFGT: biological systems may be described through regimes where closure is partial and ordinary complete closure is not the target. This is not an identity, derivation, or typed IFGT/Bio translation, and it does not complete any upstream VED/IFGT bridge. The statement does not itself establish a biological maintenance mechanism or the relevant region's existence.

\subsection{Status of Time}

The paper uses external time \(t\) and introduces internal time \(\tau\) as an interpretive variable. This separates observed time from the progress of internal state update. A precise operational definition of \(\tau\) is outside the scope of the present paper.

\subsection{Limits}

Several limits should be stated. The framework does not derive the parameter set \(\theta\) for a concrete organism. The explicit forms of \(\I\), \(\J\), and \(\PhiI\) are system-dependent, and no operational biological measurement map is supplied. The paper does not provide numerical predictions or test its toy outputs against an external biological dataset; cited literature provides background rather than a paired model--data comparison.

The framework is therefore a proposed common description, not a detailed or empirically calibrated model of a particular biological system.

\subsection{Future Directions}

Future work should identify \(\I\), \(\J\), and \(\PhiI\) in concrete systems, analyze the structure of parameter spaces, and quantify attractor regions. For intelligence, further work must address memory, temporal integration, language, and interaction among individuals.

\subsection{Summary}

The framework describes biological phenomena in a single formal language without using design or purpose as primitives. It does not address all mechanistic detail or provide numerical prediction. It is best understood as a basis for description and later specialization.
