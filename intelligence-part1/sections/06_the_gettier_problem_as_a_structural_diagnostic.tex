\section{The Gettier Problem as a Structural Diagnostic}

\noindent\textbf{Scope of the account.} Knowledge as a provisional stopping structure is a local definition. Reading Gettier cases through that definition offers a structural diagnostic, not independent confirmation of the framework or a proof that every static repair is impossible. Case classification independent of this interpretation, and independent criteria for closure and stopping, remain unresolved.


\subsection{Overview}

The Gettier problem is usually treated as a problem in the definition of knowledge. This paper reads it differently. It is used as a structural diagnostic of the distinction between a stopping structure and a proposed dynamical property.

The classical definition of knowledge as justified true belief attempts to stabilize knowledge by adding conditions. Gettier cases show that the stabilization fails. The suggested interpretation uses the model's distinction between knowledge as a stopping structure and intelligence as ongoing dynamics; it does not independently explain the failure.

\subsection{Knowledge as stopping structure}

Knowledge is useful because it stops. A proposition can be stored, cited, transmitted, and reused only because it has become sufficiently stable within an observational window.

This stability is also the source of the problem. A knowledge state can be true, believed, and justified while still being dynamically fragile. It may fail under counterfactual variation, under later revision, or under a wider inferential context.

The point is not that knowledge is useless. The point is that knowledge cannot carry the full dynamical property of intelligence.

\subsection{Why Gettier cases recur}

On the reading proposed here, a Gettier case compares a stopped state satisfying available criteria with a dynamic guarantee attributed to those criteria. A repair can be described as adding a stopping condition; this description does not decide whether that repair succeeds.

The Gettier problem can thus be read through a possible mismatch:

\begin{quote}
A stopping structure is being asked to guarantee an ongoing motion.
\end{quote}

Whether any particular refinement can address a Gettier case is not decided by this reinterpretation; the general impossibility of all repairs is not established.

\subsection{Relation to the four-layer decomposition}

In the decomposition of \S{}2, knowledge is the layer of stability, whereas intelligence is the layer of revisable motion. Reading Gettier cases through that distinction is a diagnostic proposal, not independent confirmation of the decomposition.

Within this decomposition, knowledge and intelligence have distinct roles. Intelligence is described as generating and using knowledge without being identical with it. Whether stable stopping structures support sustained intelligent dynamics requires a criterion beyond this naming.

\subsection{Implications for later sections}

The Gettier problem prepares the argument of \S{}7 and \S{}8. If correctness cannot stabilize intelligence, then accumulated correctness may not only fail to help; it may intensify instability. \S{}7 discusses a branching hypothesis. \S{}8 distinguishes a model of internal completion from general cognitive stopping.

\subsection{Summary}

\begin{quote}
The Gettier problem is not primarily a defect in the definition of knowledge. It is a signal that knowledge and intelligence occupy different structural layers. Knowledge stops. Intelligence revises.
\end{quote}
