\noindent\textbf{Scope of the account.} Model-local scope. This paper defines intelligence as ongoing revisable motion. Its three conditions are a proposed lower bound, not a general necessity theorem. The Physarum and Transformer comparisons do not independently validate that bound. Gettier is used as a diagnostic reinterpretation; graph growth, cognitive branching, and stopping failure remain separate claims. Externalized stopping is an architecture proposal, not a universal consequence of the formal discussion.

Intelligence has often been discussed as an ability, a property, an accumulated body of knowledge, or a possession of correctness. This paper contrasts possession-based descriptions with a model-local definition of intelligence as ongoing revisable dynamics.

The paper proposes three minimal axiomatic conditions: a connected state space, temporal ordering, and a global constraint $\Phi$. These conditions are not presented as a complete theory of intelligence, but as a lower bound on the kind of dynamics required for intelligence-like motion. They distinguish perception, recognition, knowledge, and intelligence as different structural layers. The paper then compares two radically different substrates, the reticulate dynamics of \emph{Physarum polycephalum} and the layer transitions of Transformer architectures, and reads both through the same structural conditions. In particular, the flow-reinforcement dynamics of the Physarum solver formulated by Tero, Kobayashi, and Nakagaki (2007) provides a role comparison for $\Phi$ as a proposed global bias; it does not independently validate irreducibility to local update rules.

From this structure, the Gettier problem is reinterpreted not as a defect in the definition of knowledge, but as a mismatch between a stopping structure and the dynamical property it is asked to guarantee. Correctness, connectivity, cognitive branching, and instability are separated as distinct claims. The proposed connection among them is not a derived cognitive mechanism. The internal-stopping discussion concerns a chosen model of completion, not all individual cognitive stopping.

The local interpretive boundary of this stopping model is named the \emph{differential horizon of intelligence}. This is not a second or separate Differential Horizon beyond VED Vol.4. It is a local horizon-form: a proposed analogous boundary within the observational window of intelligence. The paper situates this horizon-form within a four-layer correspondence spanning the physical layer (VED Vol.4), the biological layer (Bio-IFGT), the consciousness layer, and the intelligence layer, while leaving the detailed structure of externalized stopping mechanisms to subsequent work.
