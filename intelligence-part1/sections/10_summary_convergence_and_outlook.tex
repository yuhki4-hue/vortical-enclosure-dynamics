\section{Summary, Convergence, and Outlook}

\noindent\textbf{Scope of the account.} This summary preserves the distinction between local definitions, proposed structural conditions, and untested cognitive consequences. The six constraints are not a proved set of universal necessary conditions. Cross-domain examples motivate comparison but do not independently validate the mapping. Neither graph growth nor the formal stopping model establishes universal individual closure failure. Externalization remains a proposal; its effectiveness and operational criteria are open.


\subsection{Overview}

The preceding sections formulated structural constraints on intelligence-like dynamics. This final section summarizes those constraints, describes comparisons with independently studied systems, and clarifies the scope of subsequent work.

\subsection{Summary of constraints}

\textbf{Constraint 1: Intelligence is not a stopping structure.}
Intelligence is not an ability, possession, or stored body of knowledge. It is a dynamical state that persists only under structural conditions.

\textbf{Constraint 2: Intelligence as motion is distinguished from stored records.}
What can be stored are stopping products: knowledge, records, logs, and stabilized representations. Within the local definition, intelligence denotes ongoing dynamics, not the stored record; general storage impossibility is not established.

\textbf{Constraint 3: Three simultaneous conditions are proposed.}
Connected state space, temporal ordering, and global constraint $\Phi$ define the proposed model conditions, without establishing necessity for intelligence in general.

\textbf{Constraint 4: Correctness and stability are distinct questions.}
Correctness, reuse, connectivity, and cognitive branching are related by a hypothesis, not a derived chain.

\textbf{Constraint 5: Individual closure is considered under a chosen stopping model.}
The internal-completion model is distinct from cognitive cessation. Its applicability and the need for external stabilization remain open.

\textbf{Constraint 6: Crossing the local horizon-form is an architectural proposal.}
The differential horizon of intelligence is crossed through log, response, delay, agreement, and distributed responsibility. These do not guarantee correctness; their contribution to stopping, persistence, and later revision is proposed, not established.

These are proposed conditions within the model, not universal necessary or sufficient conditions. A system that satisfies them is not automatically highly intelligent. The instability claims remain hypotheses, not a classification of all other theories.

\subsection{The convergence of independent research lines}

The structural argument compares several independent research paths through its own role vocabulary; this does not independently validate the proposed mapping.

The Physarum research lineage begins from biophysical observation. It describes adaptive behavior through flow reinforcement, decay, and global boundary conditions without attributing intelligence as a possessed property.

Transformer architectures begin from engineering optimization. Their attention mechanisms and layer structure nevertheless instantiate connected state space, ordered updates, and global training constraints.

The present paper begins from structural analysis. The convergence of these paths does not prove a final theory. It suggests that the same minimal constraints recur across substrates.

\subsection{Outlook}

This paper is not the completion of a theory of intelligence. It is the first part of an intelligence-layer analysis within the VED/IFGT series.

Subsequent work must address:

\begin{itemize}
\item externalized stopping mechanisms;
\item log-based persistence;
\item distributed revisability;
\item social and institutional structures;
\item collective decision-making;
\item AI safety problems, including paperclip-type runaway optimization;
\item the transition dynamics among biological, consciousness, intelligence, and social layers.
\end{itemize}

The consciousness paper is complementary. It treats temporal non-closure and log referencing inside the individual window. The present paper explores a proposed boundary for externalizing stopping resources. Bio-IFGT supplies the biological quasi-closure background, while VED Vol.4 supplies the parent principle of the \DH{}.

\subsection{Concluding remarks}

This paper is a structural paper. It offers minimal axioms, structural constraints, and a naming of the local horizon-form that appears at the intelligence layer. It is not a complete definition of intelligence, not an implementation protocol, not a normative theory, and not a replacement for cognitive science or AI research.

The minimal statement is:

\begin{quote}
Intelligence is defined here as ongoing revisable motion. The proposed structural conditions, cognitive interpretation of nonclosure, and externalization architecture have separate burdens. The differential horizon of intelligence names the model-local boundary; it does not certify general cognitive impossibility.
\end{quote}

In the broader series, VED Vols.1--4 formulate the physical differential-development structure, Bio-IFGT formulates biological quasi-closure, the consciousness paper formulates log referencing at the consciousness layer, and the present paper formulates the minimal structure and local horizon-form of intelligence. Part II will address the social layer in which externalized stopping mechanisms are constructed.
