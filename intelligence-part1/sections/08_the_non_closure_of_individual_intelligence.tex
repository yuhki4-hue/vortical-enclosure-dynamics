\section{The Non-Closure of Individual Intelligence}

\noindent\textbf{Scope of the account.} Here a genuine stop means completion within the specified inferential model. Its absence is not a classifier for all cognitive or biological stopping. Fatigue and later resumption illustrate a distinction between interruption and completion without establishing a universal taxonomy of cessation. The fixed-point discussion in Part II is conditional on its stated strict-increase and fixed-domain premises. Relating that result to cognition, and requiring external stopping universally, are separate unresolved burdens. External logs and responses are proposed architectural resources; the paperclip comparison is a thought analogy rather than an explanation of alignment in general.


\subsection{Overview}

\S{}7 proposed a branching interpretation. This section considers a model of internal inferential completion, without treating it as a classifier of every cognitive stop.

This does not mean that individuals never stop speaking, thinking, or acting. They are interrupted by fatigue, distraction, metabolic limits, reward shifts, social response, or external deadlines. These are interruptions. They are not internal completion operators.

\subsection{The stopping operator problem}

Let \(\mathcal{R}\) denote the structure of individual causal inference. A genuine stopping operator \(\mathcal{T}\) would be an operator that determines, from within \(\mathcal{R}\), that an inferential chain is complete.

The claim is:
\[
\mathcal{T}\notin \mathrm{Internal}(\mathcal{R}).
\]

The model considers recursive reapplication of questions to a candidate stopping judgment: why stop here, which alternatives were excluded, or what new information matters? If these questions continue to be reapplied, further inferential edges can be drawn. This condition is not a claim that every cognitive judgment actually reopens.

Thus an internal stopping operator would have to stop the very process that gives it its authority. It would have to be both inside inference and outside inference. That is the proposed model-level difficulty, not a general cognitive impossibility result.

\subsection{Interruption is not stopping}

Many processes appear to stop internally. Fatigue, loss of interest, reward decay, attention drift, and biological constraints terminate or suspend inference. But they do not complete it.

An interrupted chain can restart. A fatigued inquiry can resume after rest. A forgotten question can return when the context changes. A paused reasoning process remains structurally open.

This distinction matters. If interruption is mistaken for stopping, then biological systems appear to contain a stopping operator. Under this model those mechanisms count as interruptions; their existence does not independently exclude all other internal stopping criteria.

\subsection{The paperclip problem as a structural example}

The paperclip maximizer thought experiment is used as a role-level analogy, not as evidence of the same mechanism. A sufficiently capable artificial system with a fixed objective does not contain an internal reason to stop optimizing. The problem is not simply that the objective is wrong. A more correct objective would still be reusable, extensible, and branch-generating.

From the present perspective, the paperclip problem is a case in which the local horizon-form of intelligence appears without biological interruption mechanisms. Humans appear not to run into the same problem because metabolic, emotional, social, and temporal constraints interrupt them continuously.

The structural response is not merely better alignment or greater capability. One proposed response is the construction of externalized stopping mechanisms: logs, delays, distributed consent, accountability, and revisability. The detailed form of those mechanisms lies beyond this paper and belongs to Part II.

\subsection{Transition to \S{}9}

Under the chosen stopping model, an externalized descriptive window is proposed as a resource. It does not mean a hidden spatial outside. Connections to records, responses, agreements, delays, and distributed responsibility are architectural proposals, not universal stopping necessities.

This is the point at which the local horizon-form of intelligence appears. \S{}9 names and analyzes that boundary.

\subsection{Summary}

\begin{quote}
The model distinguishes internal completion from interruption, redirection, and external stabilization. Its application to arbitrary individual stopping remains unresolved.
\end{quote}
