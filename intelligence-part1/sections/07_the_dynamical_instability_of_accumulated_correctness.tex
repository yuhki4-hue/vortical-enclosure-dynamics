\section{The Dynamical Instability of Accumulated Correctness}

\noindent\textbf{Scope of the account.} Four burdens must be distinguished: graph counting under suitable graph conditions; mapping correctness to connectivity; identifying actual cognitive branches; and the proposed psychological consequences. The displayed relations retain their existing form but are schematic where propositions are related to fields. No proposition-to-field evaluation or cognitive graph extraction is defined here. Edge/path growth depends on graph and counting conditions not fixed by the notation alone. Instability and failure of stopping are qualitative hypotheses, not measured or necessary consequences of the curves.


\subsection{Overview}

\S{}6 showed that knowledge cannot substitute for intelligence. This section proposes a stronger dynamical hypothesis:

\begin{quote}
Accumulated correctness may increase branching demands under the proposed interpretation; cognitive destabilization is not a derived consequence.
\end{quote}

The graph description is not a psychological result. Reusing correctness as connectivity is a proposed representation; its connection to cognitive branching and stopping is a separate hypothesis.

\subsection{Why correctness increases connectivity}

A proposition treated as correct becomes reusable. It can serve as a premise, connect to other propositions, generate consequences, support analogies, and become a node in further inquiry.

In IFGT vocabulary, accumulated knowledge increases local information density:
\[
I(s)\uparrow.
\]
Under the proposed graph correspondence, the increase in \(I\) is read as related to branching of information flow, rather than as a derived cognitive consequence:
\[
\nabla\cdot J \uparrow.
\]

The more correct stopping structures a system accumulates, the more inferential paths become available.

\subsection{Why correctness contains no stopping condition}

Correctness does not stop inference. It justifies further inference.

If a proposition \(p\) is correct, then several questions become structurally legitimate: why is \(p\) correct, where does it apply, what are its exceptions, how does it relate to other correct propositions, and what follows from it?

Thus correctness functions as a branching source:
\[
\mathrm{correctness}(p)\Rightarrow \nabla\cdot J|_p\neq 0.
\]
The state \(\nabla\cdot J=0\) is not excluded merely by a proposition's reuse; the printed implication above remains a schematic, formally unresolved relation.

\subsection{Branching explosion}

The proposed chain, whose transitions require distinct support, is:

\begin{enumerate}
\item correct knowledge accumulates;
\item inferential connectivity increases;
\item branching degree increases;
\item stopping points dissolve.
\end{enumerate}

If the number of accumulated correct nodes is \(|V|\), then possible edges may grow approximately as \(|E|\sim |V|^2\). If a typical branching factor is \(b\), then paths to depth \(d\) grow as \(b^d\). These counting motifs require appropriate graph conditions and do not alone establish cognitive loss of finality.

\subsection{Why ``more intelligence'' does not solve the problem}

The natural response to branching explosion is to add more intelligence: more knowledge, faster reasoning, deeper specialization, or larger search. The proposed hypothesis is that these responses may increase branching demand rather than supply a completion criterion.

More knowledge is associated here with \(I\). How stronger reasoning, faster processing, or specialization changes branching and stopping remains a cognitive mapping question, not a consequence of graph growth alone.

This motivates separating capability from the proposed stopping architecture; it does not establish universal absence of an internal stopping operator.

\subsection{Summary}

\begin{quote}
Correctness may support further inference. Graph growth, cognitive branching, and psychological instability are distinct burdens; externalized stopping is one proposed architectural response.
\end{quote}
