\section{The Schwarzschild Solution as Exterior Closure}
\label{sec:schwarzschild}

\subsection{What This Section Does}

Section~\ref{sec:einstein} proposed a fixed-point target and a weak-field
structural comparison for gravitational dynamics.
The next step is to examine the simplest configuration:
static, spherically symmetric, and with localized closure density.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Closure concentrated at a single point.}}
\end{equation}
In general relativity, this leads to the Schwarzschild solution.
Within VED, the modified-distance model is compared with this exterior form.

\subsection{Definition of the Exterior Vacuum}

Consider a localized closure distribution $L(x) = M\,\delta(x)$.
Outside the localized region ($r > R$), $L(r) = 0$.
In this region, closure density vanishes but the metric remains influenced
by the boundary conditions imposed by the central structure.

Here the Poisson-type relation is used as an effective weak-field analogy in a
continuum scalar-field domain supporting the Laplacian. Its proportionality
coefficient, units, and boundary data belong to that effective specification;
it is not a discrete graph derivation or an exact recovery of general
relativity.
The potential equation becomes:
\begin{equation}
  \nabla^2\Psi \propto L = 0
  \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad
  \nabla^2\Psi = 0.
\end{equation}
For spherical symmetry, the solution is:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\Psi(r) = -\frac{G_{\text{eff}} M}{r}.}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{The Newtonian potential.}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Form of the Metric}

In the weak-gravity limit, the metric takes the form:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:schwarzschild_weak}
  \boxed{
    ds^2
    \approx
    -\!\left(1-\frac{2GM}{r}\right)dt^2
    +\left(1+\frac{2GM}{r}\right)dr^2
    + r^2 d\Omega^2.
  }
\end{equation}
This expression corresponds to the first-order expansion of the Schwarzschild metric.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Consistent with the Schwarzschild solution (weak-gravity limit).}}
\end{equation}
A full nonlinear recovery would require solving the fixed-point equation beyond
linear order and matching the resulting coefficients; that recovery remains
open.

\subsection{What Is Happening}

General relativity interprets this solution as curvature of spacetime.
VED interprets it differently.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Distance cost is modified by closure density.}}
\end{equation}
Within the effective interpretation, propagation paths and the metric may be
compared with bending and time-dilation behavior.  Coincidence of observable
consequences is not established here.

\subsection{VED Interpretation of the Horizon}

The Schwarzschild radius is $r_s = 2GM$.
At this radius, $g_{tt} \to 0$.
VED proposes a path-structure reading of this comparison target, rather than
establishing a physical account of geometric breakdown. The following boxed
statement names that candidate interpretation only:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{The horizon is a critical point of path structure.}}
\end{equation}
In this proposed reading, divergent path cost, frozen propagation, and a
critical closure density are candidate features of a horizon phase boundary,
as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:phase_transition}. Their dynamical realization,
stability, and physical identification are not established here.
