\section{Black Hole Interior as a Non-Equilibrium Phase}
\label{sec:black_hole}

\subsection{Revisiting the Meaning of the Horizon}

Section~\ref{sec:schwarzschild} proposed comparison of the Schwarzschild radius
$r_s=2GM$ with a critical point of the modified distance structure.
At this radius, propagation cost diverges and the exterior solution ceases to apply.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{The horizon is modeled as a candidate phase boundary.}}
\end{equation}
This interpretation differs from the conventional geometric breakdown.
The model interprets the horizon as a possible transition between two regimes
of closure dynamics.

\subsection{Exterior vs.\ Interior}

Outside the horizon, the classical phase holds:
\begin{equation}
  \sigma \approx 0, \qquad \nabla \cdot J \approx 0, \qquad \partial_t C \approx 0.
\end{equation}
In this proposed regime, closure structure, approximate conservation, and an
effective metric are treated as candidate stable features.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Exterior = classical closure phase.}}
\end{equation}
The model proposes that crossing the horizon changes these conditions.

\subsection{Reignition of $\sigma$}

For the proposed interior regime, the generation term is taken to be nonzero:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\sigma > 0.}
\end{equation}
In the model interpretation, log generation resumes and the fixed-point
description is no longer imposed: $\partial_t C\neq0$. This supplies a
candidate dynamical-geometry reading, not an established physical interior
derivation.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{A time-like accumulation process is modeled as active again.}}
\end{equation}
The interior is therefore proposed as a non-equilibrium closure regime.

\subsection{Collapse of the Fixed Point}

In the exterior, $\partial_t C \approx 0$;
in the interior, $\partial_t C \neq 0$.
The metric is no longer represented by the fixed-point target in this regime.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{The fixed-point description is taken to cease applying.}}
\end{equation}
An evolving effective-geometry interpretation is proposed rather than derived.

\subsection{The Singularity as a Closure Limit}

General relativity predicts a singularity at the center.
Within VED,
the same region is interpreted as the limit of gravitational closure.
Using the effective non-closure barrier introduced in Vol.~1,
\begin{equation}
  B(L) = -\kappa_B \log\!\left(1 - \frac{L}{L_{\max}}\right)
  \to \infty
  \qquad (L \to L_{\max}).
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Perfect closure is not an ordinary attainable state.}}
\end{equation}
Thus the center is not treated as an ordinary object of infinite density
within the VED descriptive window.
It is read as an asymptotic closure limit.
In the language developed later in Vol.~4,
this limit is a local Differential Horizon of the gravitational
description:
the point where closure attempts to erase the differences required
for the description itself.

\subsection{Interior Dynamics}

Within the proposed $\sigma>0$ regime, closure structure is modeled as being
continuously regenerated.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{The interior is a candidate structural-regeneration regime.}}
\end{equation}
Logs are generated, closure reorganizes, and geometry evolves.
The interior is therefore dynamically active rather than a state of collapse.

\subsection{The Inversion}

Conventional interpretation: black hole = endpoint.

VED:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Black hole = entry into a new phase.}}
\end{equation}
The exterior is a fixed world; the interior is a world under generation.

\subsection{Correspondence with the Early Universe}

The early-universe and black-hole-interior models both use a $\sigma>0$ regime.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
    \text{The early universe and black-hole interior are proposed as
    analogous } \sigma>0 \text{ regimes.}
  }
\end{equation}

\subsection{Conclusion}

\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{A non-singular interior is a proposed model interpretation.}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{The interior is modeled as a non-equilibrium closure phase.}}
\end{equation}
Closure evolution and dynamical effective geometry remain candidate physical
interpretations.
