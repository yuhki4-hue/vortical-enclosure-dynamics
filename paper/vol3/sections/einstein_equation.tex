\section{The Einstein Equation as a Fixed-Point Structure}
\label{sec:einstein}

\subsection{Positioning the Question}

So far: distance is derived from $C_{ij}$;
gravity appears as closure density $L$;
the metric takes the form $g_{\mu\nu} = g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu} + \alpha_G L_{\mu\nu}$.
Here $L$ names the scalar Vol.~1 closure-density / intensity quantity, while
$L_{\mu\nu}$ is the tensorial Vol.~3 quantity. They are not identical by
notation alone; the cross-carrier bridge remains separate from the equations
used in this section.
The next question is therefore unavoidable:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{What equation does this structure obey?}}
\end{equation}
In conventional physics, the Einstein equation
$G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}$
is postulated as a fundamental law.
Within VED, the situation is reversed.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{The gravitational equation is not assumed.}}
\end{equation}
Instead, the volume asks whether it can be recovered as an effective target of
the self-consistent closure structure.

\subsection{Starting Point: The Fixed-Point Equation}

Vol.~1 introduced the self-referential structure:
\begin{equation}
  C_{ij} = 1 - \exp(-\lambda \rho_i H_{ij}),
  \qquad
  \rho_i = \sum_j C_{ij}.
\end{equation}
This defines a nonlinear fixed-point problem.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Structure determines itself.}}
\end{equation}
Geometry, density, and flow are all determined simultaneously
by this self-consistency condition.
This self-referential structure motivates the proposed comparison with
gravitational field equations.

\subsection{Continuum Limit and Vorticity}

The discrete flow remains $J_{ij}=C_{ij}-C_{ji}$. At the continuum level this
section uses a field denoted by $J$; its connection to $J_{ij}$ is an informal,
open bridge because no averaging, scaling, orientation, or vector-field map is
supplied. Within that target representation:
\begin{equation}
  \omega = \nabla \times J, \qquad L \sim |\omega|.
\end{equation}
Within this continuum vector-field representation, the first relation defines
vorticity and the second is an effective proportional scalar measure. Its
magnitude convention and normalization are not fixed, and neither relation is
by itself a stability theorem.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Closure density appears as vorticity of the flow.}}
\end{equation}
This is an intended connection from microscopic closure structure to
macroscopic geometric distortion; the bridge remains open.

\subsection{Fixed-Point Condition}

In the classical phase established in Vol.~1:
\begin{equation}
  \sigma \approx 0, \qquad \nabla \cdot J \approx 0.
\end{equation}
These conditions correspond to approximate conservation.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Conservation laws are properties of the phase.}}
\end{equation}
Under the model interpretation, these conditions identify a candidate stable
regime for an effective metric.  Stability and physical gravitational geometry
are not established by the displayed conditions alone.

\subsection{Self-Consistency Condition for the Metric}

From Section~\ref{sec:closure_density},
$g_{\mu\nu} = g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu} + \alpha_G L_{\mu\nu}$.
However, $L_{\mu\nu}$ depends on flow, which depends on distance,
which depends on the metric itself.
Therefore $L_{\mu\nu} = L_{\mu\nu}[g]$, and the metric must satisfy:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:fixed_point_metric}
  \boxed{g_{\mu\nu} = g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu} + \alpha_G L_{\mu\nu}[g].}
\end{equation}
This defines a fixed-point problem for the metric.
It is a proposed self-consistency target in the effective continuum
representation. The display does not establish a solution, existence,
uniqueness, convergence of an iteration, or stability; the construction of
$L_{\mu\nu}[g]$ also remains bridge-dependent.

\subsection{Linearization and the Form of the Equation}

In the weak-gravity limit $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}$
($|h_{\mu\nu}| \ll 1$),
the following is an intended comparison target for a first-order treatment of
equation~\eqref{eq:fixed_point_metric}, not a derived expansion here:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:einstein_analog}
  \boxed{G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G_{\text{eff}} L_{\mu\nu}.}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Structurally analogous to the Einstein equation.}}
\end{equation}
The displayed equation is structurally analogous to the Einstein equation and
serves as an intended weak-field target.  Precise coefficients, a complete
linearization derivation, and nonlinear recovery are not supplied here.

\subsection{The Inversion}

Conventional understanding: the Einstein equation = a fundamental law.

VED:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
    \text{The Einstein equation is an intended weak-field target of the fixed-point structure.}
  }
\end{equation}
The proposed comparison is scoped to the classical phase.  This does not
establish that physical gravity emerges or that the Einstein equation is
recovered outside the stated approximation.

\subsection{Why General Relativity Works}

This interpretation is intended to be compatible with the weak-field success
of GR; it does not itself explain or reproduce the full empirical record.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{GR is not incorrect.}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{It is an effective theory of the classical closure phase.}}
\end{equation}
In this proposed phase description, closure density varies slowly and
conservation is treated approximately.  A quantitative comparison showing
when the effective metric reproduces Einstein dynamics remains open.
