\section{Closure Density and Geometry}
\label{sec:closure_density}

\subsection{The Quantity Called Closure Density}

Section~\ref{sec:gravity_distance} introduced $L_{ab}$ as the additional
contribution to the path cost:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{L_{ab} = \sum_k \phi_{abk}.}
\end{equation}
This represents the total contribution of triangular closures containing
the edge $(a,b)$.
Each triangle contributes to structural congestion,
and the sum measures how densely that edge is embedded in closure structure.
Intuitively, regions with many triangles are structurally dense;
regions with few triangles are structurally sparse.

\begin{remark}[Carrier-specific notation]
$L_{ab}$ is an edge quantity in this volume. It is not the scalar
$L\sim|\omega|$ of Vol.~1 and is not the tensor $L_{\mu\nu}$ introduced below.
No equality among these carriers is implied by the shared letter.
\end{remark}
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Closure density = density of structure.}}
\end{equation}
This interpretation follows the Vol.~2 model-local use of triangular closure
as the minimal candidate in its stated finite closure class.

\subsection{Why It Distorts Distance}

The modified edge cost was $w_{ab} = -\log S_{ab} + \epsilon L_{ab}$.
The first term favors highly permeable paths;
the second penalizes structurally congested regions.
Thus, large closure density increases traversal cost.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Closure density effectively stretches distance.}}
\end{equation}
This reproduces the qualitative behavior associated with gravity:
dense regions become harder to traverse,
propagation paths bend around them,
and effective geometry is distorted.
Unlike general relativity, curvature is not introduced here as a fundamental
geometric postulate.  The path modification is proposed as an effective
candidate for geometry-like distortion.

\subsection{Tensorization}

Closure density is directional.
An edge contributes differently depending on orientation.
To describe this, define a coarse-grained tensor over a region $U$:
\begin{equation}
  L_{\mu\nu}(x)
  = \left\langle L_{ab}\,\hat{e}^{(ab)}_\mu \hat{e}^{(ab)}_\nu \right\rangle_U,
\end{equation}
where $\hat{e}^{(ab)}_\mu$ is the direction vector determined internally
from the distance structure. No external embedding space is assumed.

The displayed regional average is the proposed Vol.~3 construction of the
tensorial quantity from edge data. It does not identify either quantity with
Vol.~1's scalar $L$, and it does not by itself supply a complete
discrete-to-continuum bridge. In particular, sampling and normalization rules,
scaling / limiting behavior, uniqueness of the tensorization, and the relation
to the scalar $L$ remain open; the displayed construction is a partial bridge.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{L_{\mu\nu} = \text{closure tensor.}}
\end{equation}
This tensor is intended to encode directional structural congestion within the
effective representation and can parameterize anisotropic path-cost changes.

\subsection{Relation to the Metric}

The modification of distance can be absorbed into an effective metric:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:metric_closure}
  g_{\mu\nu} = g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu} + \alpha_G L_{\mu\nu},
\end{equation}
where $g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}$ is the proposed effective background metric of Vol.~1,
$L_{\mu\nu}$ is the closure tensor,
and $\alpha_G$ is a structural coupling constant.

This relation is internal to the proposed continuum description. The bridge
from the network distance $d_{ij}$ to $g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}$ is partial and open:
no reconstruction, convergence, normalization, or uniqueness result is
supplied here.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Closure density contributes to the proposed effective metric.}}
\end{equation}
This is structurally analogous to the statement that matter curves spacetime,
but no matter field has been introduced.
Whether the effective relation identifies physical geometry remains open.

\subsection{$G$ as an Effective Coupling}

The coefficient $\alpha_G$ plays the role of gravitational coupling:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{G_{\text{eff}} \sim \alpha_G.}
\end{equation}
In this representation the constant is introduced as an effective parameter
of coarse-grained closure structure; the text does not establish whether a
corresponding physical constant is fundamental or derived.
At microscopic scales, different particle closures may contribute differently;
at macroscopic scales, coarse-graining averages these contributions.
This motivates a candidate approximately universal effective coupling; no
cross-species convergence derivation is supplied here.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Universality of } G \text{ is an open coarse-graining target.}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Summary}

Closure density is used as the model source of a candidate gravitational
distortion.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Closure density is a candidate source of gravity-like behavior.}}
\end{equation}
Under the proposed partial bridge, the modification of distance is represented
by an effective metric. Reconstruction and physical interpretation remain
open; this statement does not complete the distance-to-metric map.
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Coarse-grained closure geometry is a candidate spacetime description.}}
\end{equation}
