\section{Closure and Persistent Structure}
\label{sec:closure}

\subsection{What Is Closure}

A \textbf{closure} is a configuration in which causal flow circulates along
a loop, the loop reinforces itself through the self-referential selection
equation~\cref{eq:Cij}, and the resulting pattern is dynamically sustained.
A closure is not a boundary, but a circulation that sustains itself.

Closure is proposed as a mechanism that can support flow trapping and
maintained gradients rather than dissipation. The sustained behavior in the
model definition above is a target, not a persistence guarantee from the
self-consistency equation; its realization requires dynamical stability analysis.

\subsection{The Minimal Closure Is Triangular}

Within the finite simple-loop closure class considered here, the first
candidate nontrivial enclosure involves three nodes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Two nodes}: the only cycle $i \to j \to i$ is purely
    oscillatory, with no internal degrees of freedom and no stable phase
    structure.
  \item \textbf{Three nodes}: the cycle $i \to j \to k \to i$ admits an
    oriented loop with internal area. The three edges span a two-dimensional
    phase space, allowing stable phase relationships.
  \item \textbf{Four or more}: in the stated finite decomposition picture,
    these cycles are treated as compositions of triangular sub-loops.
\end{itemize}

\begin{keybox}
\textit{Within the stated finite simple-loop closure class, the triangle is
the minimal candidate enclosure.  Unrestricted uniqueness is not claimed.}
\end{keybox}

\subsection{Closure as a Dynamical Attractor}

Define the \textbf{closure energy} of a configuration $A$:

\begin{equation}
  E(A)
  = \alpha\left|\sum_{i\in A}\vec{e}_i\right|^2
  - \beta\,\Phi(A)
  + \gamma|A|
  + B(L(A)),
  \label{eq:closure_energy}
\end{equation}

where $\vec{e}_i$ are internal flux vectors derived from $C_{ij}$,
$\Phi(A) = \sum_{i<j<k\in A}\phi_{ijk}$ is the non-degeneracy measure,
and $B(L)$ is the effective non-closure barrier of \cref{eq:barrier}.
Thus $E(A)$ is the model energy / scoring functional used to compare
configurations in this closure model; the definition of the functional does
not by itself identify one of its minima with an observed particle.

\begin{definition}
A \textbf{particle} is a local minimum of the closure energy $E(A)$.
\end{definition}

Here ``particle'' is model terminology for a closure-energy minimum.  Such a
minimum is a particle-like structural candidate; identification with an
observed particle requires a separate stability and observational map.

\subsection{Closure Distance and Mass}

The \textbf{closure distance} of a stable configuration:

\begin{equation}
  \Delta_k = L_{\max} - L_k
  \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}

measures how close the configuration lies to the complete-closure limit.
The curvature of $E$ at the minimum gives the effective mass.
Within the phenomenological barrier approximation this yields:

\begin{keybox}
\begin{equation}
  m_k
  \propto \left.\frac{d^2 E}{dL^2}\right|_{L_k}
  \sim \frac{\kappa_B}{\Delta_k^2}.
  \label{eq:mass}
\end{equation}
\end{keybox}

The displayed scaling is retained only within that phenomenological
barrier-based scope. It is not a microscopic derivation or an identification
of $m_k$ with an observed mass without additional interpretation.

Here $\kappa_B$ is an effective parameter of the projected closure-energy
description.
Vol.~4 gives a more dynamical interpretation in which the apparent barrier
can arise from rotational feedback and dissipation rather than from a
fundamental external potential.

\begin{keybox}
\textit{Within the phenomenological scaling, larger mass-like values occur
close to the complete-closure limit.
The world is made of incomplete closures.}
\end{keybox}
