\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Most physical theories begin from assumed structure:
a spacetime manifold, a Lagrangian density, or a symmetry group.
In all such cases, the framework presupposes what it seeks to explain.

VED takes a different approach.
Starting from the single statement \textit{there is difference}, the framework
proposes formal constructions and candidate interpretations for time, space,
motion, and persistence. It does not establish that these physical targets
follow necessarily from every instance of structural asymmetry.

The central object is the \textbf{causal log matrix} $C_{ij}$,
defined on a discrete network of nodes.
$C_{ij}$ captures not the total history of causal influence between nodes,
but the portion of that history that is selectively retained as persistent structure.
From $C_{ij}$, a compact chain summarizes the proposed development:

\begin{keybox}
\begin{equation}
  \text{difference}
  \;\to\; C_{ij}
  \;\to\; \rho
  \;\to\; \tau
  \;\to\; d_{ij}
  \;\to\; J
  \;\to\; \omega
  \;\to\; L
  \label{eq:chain}
\end{equation}
\end{keybox}

\noindent
See Fig.~1 for a visual summary.

The arrows in this compact display are navigation markers rather than one
repeated relation.  In the detailed construction, $C_{ij}\to\rho$ and the
discrete $C_{ij}\to J_{ij}$ relation are definitions; $\rho\to\tau$ is an
accumulation definition relative to the parameter $t$; $S_{ij}\to d_{ij}$ is
a weighted-network construction; $J_{ij}\to\mathbf J(x)$ is an OPEN
discrete-to-continuum bridge; $\mathbf J\to\omega$ is a continuum definition;
and $\omega\to L$ is an effective proportional relation.  Physical time,
space, and persistence remain proposed interpretations rather than additional
formal consequences of the arrow glyph.

This framework is not presented as a complete predictive theory, but as a
minimal structural proposal for physical organization.
It is a structural proposal: a demonstration that the minimum ingredients
required for physical structure are considerably smaller than those
assumed in current foundational approaches.
The aim is coherence, not closure.
