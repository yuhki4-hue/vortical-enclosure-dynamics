\section{Redefining Divergence}

\label{sec:divergence}

From the preceding discussion,
one conclusion becomes unavoidable.

A physical theory cannot describe the \ungenerated{} domain itself.
But it can approach its boundary.

At the limit of that approach,
divergence necessarily appears.

\medskip
\begin{quote}
Divergence is not an error to be removed.
\end{quote}
\medskip

Traditionally,
divergence has been treated as a failure of theory.

However,
this interpretation is not required.

\medskip
\begin{quote}
Divergence is the moment at which a theory reaches the limit of its domain
and registers contact with the \DH{}.
\end{quote}
\medskip

This is not metaphorical.

Divergence appears whenever a theory is pushed toward its extreme limits:
when spatial scale approaches zero,
when energy approaches infinity,
or when gravitational curvature becomes extreme.

Regardless of the specific physical context,
the same structural pattern is observed.

\medskip
\begin{quote}
Divergence necessarily appears at the boundary of the describable domain.
\end{quote}
\medskip

This is not a coincidence.

It indicates that the theory is encountering a domain
that lies beyond its descriptive capacity.

\subsection*{What Divergence Does Not Reveal}

Importantly,
divergence does not provide information about what lies beyond that boundary.

\medskip
\begin{quote}
Divergence does not reveal the structure beyond the boundary.\\
It only reflects the limit of the theory itself.
\end{quote}
\medskip

Thus,
divergence is not a means of crossing the boundary.

\medskip
\begin{quote}
Divergence does not provide a means of access beyond the boundary.\\
It marks the impossibility of extending the theory further.
\end{quote}
\medskip

From this perspective,
divergence is not failure.

\medskip
\begin{quote}
Divergence is a contact signal.
\end{quote}
\medskip

It indicates that the theory has reached the edge of its domain
and is responding to that contact.

\subsection*{Unification of Previously Separate Phenomena}

This reinterpretation unifies phenomena
that have traditionally been treated separately.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Divergence in quantum field theory
  \item Singularities in general relativity
  \item The breakdown of spacetime at the Planck scale
  \item Extreme behavior near the speed-of-light limit
\end{itemize}

These are not independent anomalies.

\medskip
\begin{quote}
They are different appearances of contact with the \DH{}.
\end{quote}
\medskip

They differ in path and in form,
but share the same underlying structure:
theory has reached the outer edge of its domain.

\subsection*{Implication for Renormalization}

At this point,
the meaning of renormalization begins to shift.

The response to this contact is not elimination, but transformation.

Rather than removing divergence,
renormalization converts the effects of \bcontact{}
into a form that can be handled within the theory.

\medskip
\begin{quote}
Divergence and renormalization are two sides of the same phenomenon.
\end{quote}
\medskip

This will be examined in detail in the next section.
