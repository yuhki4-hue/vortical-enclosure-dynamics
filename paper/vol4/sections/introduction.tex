\section{Introduction}

\label{sec:introduction}

The problem of renormalization has stood at the center of modern physics for decades.
Quantum field theory produces divergent quantities mid-calculation;
renormalization absorbs these into observable definitions and yields predictions
of extraordinary precision.
Yet the physical meaning of this procedure has never been fully explained.

This paper takes that explanatory gap as its starting point.
We do not treat divergence as a mathematical artifact to be managed,
but as a structural signal: an indication that physical theory is approaching
a boundary that it cannot cross.

We call this boundary the \DH{}.
It is the boundary at which difference first arises---the transition between
the \ungenerated{} domain, where no physical description applies,
and the \generated{} domain, where spacetime, fields, and laws come to hold.

Three claims organize this paper.
First, divergence is not failure but \bcontact{}:
it appears when theory reaches the outer edge of its descriptive domain.
Second, renormalization is not elimination but conversion:
it translates the influence of that contact into a finite internal representation.
Third, the speed-of-light limit, the Planck-scale breakdown,
and black hole singularities are not independent problems
but different modes of approach to the same boundary.

This framework does not propose a new unified theory.
It identifies a prior structural layer: the condition under which
any physical theory can hold, and makes that condition explicit
through the \DHP{}.

\VED{} (Vortical Enclosure Dynamics) is positioned within this framework
as a theory of the generation boundary itself.
It does not describe existence; it describes the conditions under which existence begins.

The paper proceeds as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:renorm_clue} examines the unease of renormalization
and reframes it as a structural clue.
Sections~\ref{sec:past}--\ref{sec:ungenerated} establish
that physics consistently describes only post-generated structure
and that the un-generated domain is in principle indescribable.
Section~\ref{sec:dh} defines the Differential Horizon.
Sections~\ref{sec:two_modes}--\ref{sec:renorm_reinterp}
develop the reinterpretation of divergence and renormalization.
Sections~\ref{sec:replacement}--\ref{sec:unification}
re-place major theories and unsolved problems within the framework.
Section~\ref{sec:principle} states the Differential Horizon Principle.
Section~\ref{sec:ved} positions VED.
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.
