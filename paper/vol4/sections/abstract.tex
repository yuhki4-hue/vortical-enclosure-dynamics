\begin{abstract}

We propose the Differential Horizon as a structural framework for reinterpreting
divergence and renormalization in modern physics.
Rather than treating divergence as a technical failure to be corrected,
we argue that it constitutes a signal of boundary contact---an indication that
a physical theory has approached the limit at which the generated structure of
spacetime gives way to the un-generated domain, where no descriptive framework applies.

From this perspective, renormalization is not an operation that eliminates infinity,
but one that converts boundary contact into a finite internal representation.
The speed-of-light limit, the Planck-scale breakdown of spacetime continuity,
and black hole singularities are re-positioned as different modes of approach
to the same boundary, rather than as independent problems requiring separate solutions.

This framework does not propose a unified theory of interactions.
Instead, it identifies a prior structural layer: the condition under which
any physical theory can hold.
The Differential Horizon Principle states that the un-generated domain is in principle
unreachable, that approach to its boundary produces divergence, and that renormalization
is the mechanism by which that contact is absorbed into theory.

VED (Vortical Enclosure Dynamics) is positioned within this framework as a theory
of the generation boundary---not a description of existence,
but of the conditions under which existence begins.

\end{abstract}
