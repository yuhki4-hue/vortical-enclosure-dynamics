\section{Experimental Reinterpretation}


This section uses the log-referencing model formalized in preceding sections to reinterpret representative existing consciousness experiments.

The experimental descriptions below are background summaries, not new results of this account. Their dedicated primary-source citation anchors remain to be verified here. The fast/slow, sedimentation, and log-referencing readings are this paper's interpretations of that background, not measurements of those model processes or experimental validation of the framework.

The aim is not to deny the experimental results.

Rather, it is to re-read what the existing experiments were showing — not through a consciousness-generation model, but through a log-referencing model across time scales.

Much of the confusion in consciousness research arises from placing processing, action, report, and consciousness on a single timeline.

Under that view, whatever appears earlier tends to be interpreted as cause, and whatever appears later as effect.

But in the framework of this paper, the system does not operate on a single clock.

The fast layer predicts, the slow layer sediments, and the plasticity gate introduces delay and viscosity between the two.

The time differences and report-inaccessibility observed in experiments therefore do not necessarily indicate the absence of consciousness.

Here they motivate a proposed distinction among processing, log-referenceability, pathway access, and report; they do not establish the necessity of this model's log conditions.

\subsection{Libet-Type Experiments}


In the background Libet-type setup considered here, subjects move a wrist or finger at a time of their choosing and report the moment at which they "decided to move," while brain activity is recorded.

The timing relation taken as background here is that a readiness potential precedes movement and the reported moment of conscious intention follows its onset; the particular experimental source for this summary has not been verified here.

Traditionally this result has often been interpreted as: the brain decides first and consciousness learns of it afterward, or alternatively, free will is an illusion.

But this interpretation presupposes that decision, brain activity, and conscious report are placed on the same timeline.

This paper does not adopt that presupposition.

The readiness potential can be read as the formation of an action candidate in the fast layer — a signal that the future-oriented predictive and motor dynamics have begun falling into a certain action attractor.

The model interprets the report of "I just decided to move" as a reportable form associated with fast processing connecting to slow-layer log referencing. This proposed sequence does not identify the readiness potential with $v_f$, or the report timestamp with a phenomenal opening.

This paper proposes to read that background timing difference as a transformation time between speed scales, rather than simply as a delay in consciousness.

Writing the fast-layer processing speed as $v_f$ and the slow-layer sedimentation speed as $v_s$:

\begin{equation}
\Delta v = v_f - v_s
\end{equation}

The local effective ansatz relates model speed difference to internal delay $\tau$; it is not a conversion calibrated from the background experiment:

\begin{equation}
\tau \propto \frac{\Delta v}{\mu}
\end{equation}

Here $\mu$ is the coupling viscosity between fast and slow layers.

In this proposed reading, the timing difference is interpreted as the delay for fast action preparation to become a reportable slow-layer log. This does not claim that the experiments directly measured $\Delta v$, $\mu$, or $\tau$ of the present model.

In this view, even the expression "consciousness is delayed" is not quite right.

The model distinguishes fast processing from the proposed opening associated with log-referenceability. It hypothesizes an opening at that transition, rather than treating a reportable timestamp as an independently identified phenomenal event.

For this reason, the earlier appearance of the readiness potential does not show that consciousness is powerless.

Within this paper's interpretation, consciousness is not action preparation itself, but the passage point at which action preparation sediments into the slow layer and becomes referenceable.

\subsection{Scope of the Free-Will Interpretation}


The methodological issue considered here is whether "decision" should be represented as a single instantaneous point. The background timing relation alone does not logically entail the absence of free will; neither does this model prove its presence.

But in the structure of this paper, decision does not localize to a single moment.

In the fast layer, multiple action candidates arise predictively.

The plasticity gate regulates which of them can sediment into the slow layer.

In the slow layer, past logs, meaning gradient, bodily state, and context compose the reportable form.

Compressing this whole into a single "decision point" loses the temporal structure.

The model therefore questions the compression implicit in asking whether "consciousness decided or the brain decided," rather than claiming that this alternative has been experimentally refuted.

The appropriate questions are: which processing preceded in the fast layer; through what delay $\tau$ did that processing connect to the slow layer; which logs were referenced by the meaning gradient $\nabla\Phi$; and at what point did a reportable form obtain.

This reframing offers a model-based way to discuss preparation and report separately. The background timing does not uniquely support it or empirically establish the failure of a single-time account.

\subsection{Split-Brain Experiments}


The background split-brain description considered here concerns restricted interhemispheric information sharing following callosal disconnection; its dedicated experimental citation anchor remains to be verified.

The background asymmetry used in this comparison is appropriate behavioral responding to stimuli presented to the right hemisphere without corresponding verbal report. It is not a new observation by this paper or a measurement of log flow.

This phenomenon has often been described as evidence for "two consciousnesses" or "two selves."

Behavioral and verbal pathway asymmetry alone does not establish that consciousness has doubled. This account supplies no independent subject-counting criterion and establishes neither one nor two phenomenal subjects.

This paper interprets the background asymmetry in terms of pathways of processing, sedimentation, and report.

Even if processing occurs in the right hemisphere, if that processing does not reach the left-hemisphere-dominant verbal reporting pathway, the subject cannot explicitly articulate it.

This does not mean the processing is absent.

The background behavioral response is taken as an indication of processing, not a phenomenal criterion. The model interprets the reporting asymmetry as a possible failure of integration into reportable slow logs or access to them. A blocked log-reference route is thus a candidate account, not an observed mediator.

This point corresponds to Libet-type experiments.

In Libet-type experiments, a temporal delay appears between processing and report.

In split-brain experiments, a pathway disconnection appears between processing and report.

In both cases, this paper's interpretation invokes slow-layer log sedimentation and referenceability as conditions for explicit consciousness; that interpretation is not an experimental measurement of log sedimentation or a validation of these model conditions.

Here ``explicit consciousness'' denotes a candidate model reading of access and report, not a universal phenomenal necessity or the absence of consciousness whenever report is unavailable.

\subsection{Reporting Bottleneck and Log Accessibility}


What is particularly important in split-brain experiments is not conflating reportability with consciousness.

Reportability is the pathway by which part of the conscious state is externalized.

But inability to report does not mean processing has not occurred.

Within this account, explicit report is modeled as requiring the following three conditions. They are local report/access conditions, not established universal necessities or a criterion for phenomenal consciousness:

First, processing has occurred in the fast layer.

Second, that processing has sedimented into the slow layer and become log-referenceable.

Third, that log is connected to the verbal or behavioral reporting pathway.

The model reads the background split-brain asymmetry in terms of the third condition; the log-to-path mapping itself has not been operationally established.

As a result, certain processing appears as behavior but not as verbal report.

There is no need to read this asymmetry as "two consciousnesses."

The proposed interpretation distinguishes a reporting-path asymmetry from its hypothetical log-reference mediator, without using either to count phenomenal subjects.

\subsection{Rationalization as Log Completion}


The background example considered here is a retrospective explanation of an action whose generating information was not accessible to the reporting hemisphere. Its dedicated experimental source remains to be verified; this description does not itself establish a slow-log completion process.

This example is discussed here under the labels "confabulation" or "rationalization."

In the position of this paper, this rationalization is not deliberate falsification.

The model offers a candidate account in which slow-log referencing maintains coherence by filling inaccessible log gaps. This is a completion hypothesis, not a mechanism demonstrated by the background example.

The model assigns the slow layer a role in maintaining coherent self-related logs. Their relation to experienced self-continuity is a hypothesis, not a derivation of phenomenal selfhood. This maintenance reading leaves first-log initialization and ownership of the logs open.

But when the processing pathway is severed, actions occur whose generating logs cannot be referenced.

In this candidate account, the system is modeled as constructing a coherent explanation from referenceable logs rather than retaining the gap unchanged.

This rationalization does not show that consciousness is lying.

In this paper's interpretation, consciousness constructs coherence from referenceable logs.

This interpretation questions an ego with complete access; the background example does not independently establish this model or the number or nature of phenomenal subjects.

The ego is not a center that knows everything.

It names a model transformation role involving referenceable logs; its association with experienced self-continuity is proposed, not an identity established by this example.

\subsection{Experimental Correspondence to the Model}


Libet-type experiments and split-brain experiments appear on the surface to address different problems.

The former concerns temporal difference; the latter concerns interhemispheric pathway disconnection.

The model compares them through temporal and pathway aspects of its proposed report architecture, without claiming that the experiments establish a common log mechanism.

This paper schematically reinterprets the Libet-type background timing through the following relation:

\begin{equation}
\text{fast preparation} \rightarrow \text{delayed sedimentation} \rightarrow \text{reportable timestamp}
\end{equation}

This paper schematically reinterprets the split-brain background asymmetry through the following relation:

\begin{equation}
\text{localized processing} \rightarrow \text{blocked transmission} \rightarrow \text{asymmetric reportability}
\end{equation}

In both cases, processing itself and conscious report are not identical.

Gate, delay, sedimentation, and pathway access are proposed conditions for explicit access/report in this account. The phrase ``explicit consciousness'' in this interpretation does not supply a necessary condition for phenomenal consciousness itself.

Using this structure, the existing experiments are repositioned as follows:

\begin{itemize}
\item Libet-type experiments: timing reinterpreted through speed-scale difference and internal delay $\tau$, not their direct measurement.
\item Split-brain experiments: asymmetrization of log-referencing pathway and reporting pathway.
\item Rationalization: slow-layer coherence maintenance in response to inaccessible log gaps.
\item Conscious report: externalization of sedimented logs, not processing itself.
\end{itemize}

These examples motivate the model's distinction among processing, retention, access, and report. Associating log-referenceability with conscious opening remains a hypothesis, not a consequence established by the two experiments.

\subsection{What These Experiments Do Not Show}


Finally, what these experiments do not show should be made explicit.

The Libet-type timing relation described here does not by itself logically entail the absence of free will. Its interpretation in terms of model time scales remains separate from that background timing.

Likewise, the split-brain asymmetry described here alone does not establish either one or two phenomenal selves.

Log-referencing asymmetry is this paper's interpretation of the background pathway and reporting asymmetry.

Rationalization did not show that consciousness always speaks falsely.

Coherent slow-layer completion of inaccessible logs is this paper's interpretation of that background phenomenon.

These experiments therefore do not erase consciousness.

Rather, this account proposes a log-referenceable opening as a way to interpret the examples, without treating that position as experimentally established.

\subsection{Summary}


This section reinterpreted Libet-type and split-brain experiments from the log-referencing model of this paper.

Common to both is the non-identity of processing and conscious report.

Processing, log retention, and pathway access are distinguished in the proposed explicit-access/report model. Neither reportability nor behavior is used as an independent phenomenal criterion.

What this reinterpretation proposes, rather than reports as an experimental finding, is an account of temporal and pathway conditions for consciousness, not its powerlessness.
