\section{Cross-Domain Homology}

\noindent\textbf{Scope of the account.} All tables below compare lexical or functional roles. Shared symbols, arrows, and emergence forms do not establish formal homology, identity, a common mechanism, or typed translation. The field/network and mobility/error relations retain their unresolved stronger status. Additional Japanese columns and comparisons are not automatically inherited by the English draft. Historical entries are proposed operating forms rather than independently validated history.

Across the tables below, LR and LE belong to the preserved comparative architecture vocabulary. Section 5's active support-role criteria and system-boundary assessments are not transferred merely by sharing a row or a pattern. In particular, a Transformer profile or social comparison does not identify a common carrier, certify a role, or resolve unknown evidence.


The paper repeatedly uses homology as a proposed comparison rather than an identity. Physical particles, organisms, conscious states, intelligent stopping layers, and social institutions are arranged by analogous formal roles; this does not establish that they instantiate one carrier, mechanism, or typed cross-domain dynamics.
