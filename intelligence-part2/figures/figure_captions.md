## Fig.1 — The C-Landscape and the Inference Trajectory

### (i) One-sentence summary

Inference keeps moving on the closure landscape along the gradient `∇C`, but structurally it cannot settle anywhere by itself; this is the geometric expression of the non-closure theorem.

### (ii) How to read the elements

- **Horizontal axis `𝒳` and vertical axis `C`**: `𝒳` is the state space of possible inferential states. `C` is closure degree, the degree to which a state can operate as stable or stoppable.
- **Black curve, the C-landscape**: The landscape of conceptual space. High regions indicate locally stable zones; low or steep regions indicate unresolved zones with strong inferential drive; saddle-like regions mark possible branching points.
- **Green local maximum**: A point that looks as if inference might stop there locally.
- **Red dashed boundary `C_max`**: The complete-closure boundary. Because the VED term diverges as `C -> C_max`, this boundary is approached but not reached.
- **Blue trajectory `x_0 -> x_n -> x_{n+k}`**: Iterations of the inference operator `F`. The trajectory can pass through a local maximum, but it does not settle there.

### (iii) Theoretical implication

The proof card expresses Theorem 3.1. If a stopping point `x*` satisfied `F(x*) = x*`, the monotonicity of justification would require `J(F(x*)) > J(x*)`; but the fixed-point assumption gives `J(x*) > J(x*)`, a contradiction. Therefore there is no internal fixed point.

The lower formula, `for all x: F(x) != x`, must be read with the qualification "within fixed `θ`, movement type A." The theorem applies inside a fixed movement region `Ω_θ`. In §5, `μ_θ` changes the region itself, movement type B, allowing a different form of stopping to emerge as `L_E`. The figure therefore establishes the first center of the paper: `L_F` alone does not close.

## Fig.2 — Responsive and Silent Regimes as Threshold Phenomena

### (i) One-sentence summary

Responsive and silent worlds are not ontological kinds of world; they are threshold-dependent phases determined by the boundary moved by `μ_θ`.

### (ii) How to read the elements

- **Shared background band**: Both panels refer to the same environment and the same C-landscape. The difference lies in the threshold boundary, not in two separate worlds.
- **Orange dashed threshold boundary**: The minimum line at which response counts as closing the loop. It is not a fixed wall; it moves through `μ_θ`.
- **Orange slider `μ_θ`**: Threshold mobility. The arrows indicate that the boundary can move.
- **Left panel, responsive phase**: The loop `observer -> world -> observer` closes. The world returns response, feedback, and correction. Local stopping is possible.
- **Right panel, silent phase**: The return arrow is broken. The observer can output inference, but the world does not return a closing response.
- **Upper card**: The figure's reading rule: responsive/silent is a phase distinction, not an ontological division.

### (iii) Theoretical implication

This figure visualizes the starting point of the silent-world argument. In the responsive phase, the world itself supplies external mutual constraint `H_ij` and therefore local stopping. In the silent phase, that supply is missing, creating pressure to externalize stopping elsewhere.

The note that `μ_θ` does not create a new world is important. It moves the threshold through which the same world becomes response-capable. This keeps Fig.2 consistent with Fig.1: the non-closure theorem holds inside fixed `θ`, while stopping can emerge when `μ_θ` reconfigures the response threshold.

## Fig.3 — Mutual Constraint `H_ij` and Threshold Mobility `μ_θ`

### (i) One-sentence summary

Stopping regions are not generated by an isolated inference layer; they are projected through mutual constraint `H_ij`, and `μ_θ` is the ability to reorganize where stopping can be installed.

### (ii) How to read the elements

- **Large dashed ellipse, mutual constraint field `H_ij`**: The coupled field of observer-environment or inter-agent constraints. It determines where stopping can occur.
- **Agent `i`**: Contains log layer `L_R(i)`, traces of inference, and recursive comparison.
- **Agent `j` / Environment**: Contains response traces, environmental feedback, and social or material constraint. The same box can represent another agent or the environment.
- **Gray bidirectional arrows**: Mutual reference between `L_R(i)` and `L_R(j)`. Solid and dashed links indicate that coupling can be direct, delayed, or uneven.
- **Central `μ_θ` box**: Threshold mobility: movement type B, the reconfiguration of the boundary of `Ω_θ`, selecting where a stopping operator may be installed.
- **Downward orange projection arrow**: Projection of a stopping region. Stopping is not fabricated above and dropped below; it is projected as a function of mutual constraint.
- **Lower `L_E` box**: Emergent stopping layer. Stopping is generated as a relation among logs, constraint, and projected thresholds.
- **Projected stopping loci card**: Historical examples of projection targets: environment/animism, God/centralized operator, religion/maintenance, science/revision.
- **Reading-rule card**: Inference is not stopped by inference alone; it is stopped through mutual constraint. Freedom requires movable stops.

### (iii) Theoretical implication

Fig.3 compresses the second center of the paper into a mechanism diagram. The historical forms later treated as animism, God, religion, science, and social blockchain are different solutions to the same structural question: where can stopping be projected?

The decisive point is that this does not violate Fig.1. Since isolated `L_F` has no internal stopping point, stopping must be projected through `L_R` and `H_ij` and must emerge as `L_E`. The phrase "freedom requires movable stops" gives the broadest implication: freedom is not absence of stopping, but the ability to move and revise stopping loci.

## Fig.4 — The Three-Layer Architecture of Intelligence

### (i) One-sentence summary

Intelligence consists of the inference layer `L_F`, the log layer `L_R`, and the emergent stopping layer `L_E`; `L_F` cannot stop by itself, but `L_R` under external mutual constraint gives rise to `L_E`.

### (ii) How to read the elements

- **`L_F`, inference layer**: The layer on which the inference operator `F` acts along `∇C`. It has no internal fixed point.
- **`L_R`, log layer**: Traces left by `L_F` and recursive reference to those traces. It is inside the system but outside the current operation of `F`.
- **`L_E`, emergent stopping layer**: A stopping attractor that emerges from `L_R` under `H_ij`.
- **Vertical flow**: `L_F` leaves traces; traces form `L_R`; recursive log motion under mutual constraint gives rise to `L_E`.
- **Purple `μ_θ` channel**: The opening window through which region reconfiguration and stopping-region projection become possible.
- **Bottom chain**: The four-step emergence mechanism: reconfiguration, projection of stopping region, convergence of `L_R`, emergence of `L_E`.

### (iii) Theoretical implication

Fig.4 joins the first center of the paper, non-closure of `L_F`, with the second center, emergent stopping through `L_E`. The paradox is that the inability of `L_F` to stop is not merely a limit. Because it keeps moving, it leaves traces; because traces accumulate, `L_R` forms; because `L_R` can move recursively under constraint, `L_E` can emerge.

The figure also separates intelligence in general from sapiens-like intelligence. `L_F/L_R/L_E` is the general structure; endogenous `μ_θ` is the additional acceleration and reconfiguration capacity characteristic of sapiens-like intelligence.

## Fig.5 — Three Examples of VED-Defined Emergence

### (i) One-sentence summary

Emergence is not mere complexity; it is the formation of an upper attractor from lower non-closed motion under external mutual constraint, schematically `U = Φ(L, H_ij)`.

### (ii) How to read the elements

- **Top reading card**: The operational definition of VED emergence: lower motion plus mutual constraint gives rise to an upper attractor.
- **Physics column**: Lower causal logs and internal vectors under closure conditions give rise to particle-like attractors.
- **Life column**: Information flow and state development under DNA-derived constraints and non-equilibrium exchange give rise to the life attractor `Ω_life`.
- **Intelligence column**: Recursive log layer `L_R` and inference traces under observer-environment constraint give rise to the emergent stopping layer `L_E`.
- **Converging arrows**: All three cases converge into the same emergence kernel.
- **Central lower ellipse**: The VED-defined emergence formula `U = Φ(L, H_ij)`.

### (iii) Theoretical implication

Fig.5 places Intelligence Part II inside the broader VED series. The point is not that physics, life, and intelligence are the same object. Rather, the same emergence grammar repeats across layers.

The figure also separates VED emergence from vague complexity talk. Emergence requires two components: lower non-closed motion and external mutual constraint. Without motion, nothing is generated; without constraint, no attractor stabilizes. The emergent stopping layer `L_E` is therefore one instance of a larger VED pattern.

## Fig.6 — `Φ_ext ≡ Φ_net`

### (i) One-sentence summary

What appears to an individual as an external reference field `Φ_ext` is the network `Φ_net` formed by mutual synchronization among individual `L_E` layers.

### (ii) How to read the elements

- **Outer dashed ellipse, `Φ_ext`**: The common reference field as it appears from the standpoint of a single individual: norm, truth, God, monetary value, or shared reference.
- **Inner panel, `Φ_net`**: The internal structure of that appearance: an inter-individual network of emergent stopping layers.
- **`L_E` nodes**: Each node is an agent's emergent stopping layer. Color differences indicate individual variation.
- **Gray links**: Mutual reference and synchronization among `L_E` nodes.
- **External view card**: From the outside, the structure appears as a common reference field beyond the individual.
- **Internal view card**: When resolved structurally, it is a network of interacting `L_E` nodes.
- **Equation box**: `Φ_ext ≡ Φ_net`: two readings of the same stopping relation.

### (iii) Theoretical implication

Fig.6 extends Fig.3 from a single agent to an inter-individual scale. An external field does not have to be assumed first. Instead, distributed mutual constraint among agents produces the appearance of an external field.

This does not deny `Φ_ext`. It reconstructs it immanently. Gods, norms, truth, and monetary value are not dismissed as illusion; they are read as objective appearances of distributed `L_E` synchronization. This supports the later value-neutral reading of religion, science, and society as forms of stopping-structure operation.

## Fig.7 — Evolution of Stopping Structures

### (i) One-sentence summary

Animism, God, religion, science, and social blockchain are not stages of progress but different operations on the same emergent stopping layer `L_E`: installation, compression, maintenance, revision, and synchronization.

### (ii) How to read the elements

- **Horizontal axis, local to distributed**: The distribution of stopping operations.
- **Vertical axis, implicit to explicit**: The explicitness and shareability of stopping operations.
- **Bottom band, emergent stopping layer `L_E`**: The common ground on which all historical forms operate.
- **Curved trajectory**: A historical movement toward more explicit and distributed stopping operations.
- **Animism / Installation**: Stopping operators are installed into environmental loci.
- **God / Compression**: Distributed stopping points are compressed into a centralized reference.
- **Religion / Maintenance**: Ritual, canon, and community maintain stopping structures across generations.
- **Science / Revision**: Stopping points become methodically movable and revisable.
- **Social Blockchain / Synchronization**: Shared stopping points are synchronized through distributed consensus.
- **Reading-rule card**: This is not a progress narrative. `L_E` diversifies; it is not simply replaced.

### (iii) Theoretical implication

Fig.7 turns the static list of projected stopping loci into a historical map of operations. Later forms do not erase earlier forms. They add new ways of operating stopping structures.

The lower legend, "stopping is not the negation of freedom," is central. Open-ended inference requires stopping structures; without them, inference collapses into regress. The most open intelligence requires the most sophisticated stopping operations, because freedom in finite systems requires movable and revisable stops.

## Fig.8 — The Three-Axis Phase Diagram of Intelligence

### (i) One-sentence summary

Stable intelligence exists only in a narrow region between divergence and rigidity in the phase space of inference velocity `v_inf`, resolution `r_res`, and stopping hardness `η`.

### (ii) How to read the elements

- **Three axes**: `v_inf` is inferential velocity, `r_res` is resolution, and `η` is stopping hardness.
- **Origin `(0,0,0)`**: Zero inference and zero resolution is not completion but the end of intelligence.
- **Green stable-operation region**: `μ_θ` functions and `L_E` remains revisable.
- **Blue speed-gap rupture zone**: The transition band where `Δv_intel = v_inf - v_LR` becomes critical. Inference generation outruns log reference.
- **Red divergent zone**: High speed, high resolution, and internalized stopping produce runaway optimization and single-view absolutism.
- **Rigid zone**: Excessive stopping hardness fixes `L_E` and prevents adaptation.
- **Four-fold collapse card**: Divergence proceeds through `L_F` runaway, `L_R` failure, `L_E` non-emergence, and `μ_θ` window failure.
- **Active/passive stability card**: Endogenous `μ_θ` allows active return to the stable region; systems without it are passively placed there by external conditions.

### (iii) Theoretical implication

Fig.8 answers what happens when intelligence becomes too fast or too fine-grained without sufficient stopping infrastructure. Collapse is not caused by high `v_inf` alone. It begins when `v_inf` outruns the log-reference velocity `v_LR`, making `Δv_intel` critical. If `L_R` cannot keep up, `L_E` loses its basis and `μ_θ` cannot open a reconfiguration window.

The figure's warning is descriptive, not normative. A single-node, high-speed, high-resolution, internally stopped system occupies the divergent region as a dynamical consequence. The stabilizing response is not simply to slow inference, but to raise log-reference bandwidth or open stopping to external mutual constraint.

## Fig.9 — Cross-Domain Homology

### (i) One-sentence summary

The same pattern, incomplete closure → emergent attractor → sustainable dynamics, appears across physics, life, consciousness, intelligence, and society.

### (ii) How to read the elements

- **Central homology kernel**: The shared pattern of incomplete closure, emergent attractor, and sustainable dynamics.
- **Concentric dashed field**: The field in which the five layers share the same kernel.
- **Physics / VED card**: Difference, gradients, flow, vortices, quasi-closure, and particle-like attractors.
- **Life / Bio-IFGT card**: Information flow, state development, and the life attractor `Ω_life`.
- **Consciousness card**: Speed-scale difference, log-reference window, SSO/TCA non-closure, and consciousness as state rather than generated object.
- **Intelligence / Part II card**: Inference operator `F`, non-closure theorem, and emergent stopping layer `L_E`.
- **Society / `Φ_net` card**: Shared stopping structures, religion, science, institutions, consensus, and synchronization.
- **Bottom note**: Homology does not erase differences among domains.

### (iii) Theoretical implication

Fig.9 closes Intelligence Part II by connecting it to the whole VED theory tree. Fig.5 gave three examples of emergence; Fig.9 extends the map to five layers by adding consciousness and society.

The key word is homology, not identity. Particles, organisms, conscious states, intelligence, and society are not the same. They differ in implementation, scale, and dynamics. Yet they repeat the same generative grammar: non-closure gives rise to motion; constrained motion forms attractors; attractors make sustained dynamics possible. This is the non-reductionist unity of the VED framework.
