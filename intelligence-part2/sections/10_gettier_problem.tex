\section{The Gettier Problem: Stability Is Not Truth}

\noindent\textbf{Scope of the account.} The distinction between operational stability and truth is retained. Gettier is reinterpreted through that distinction, not used as independent evidence for the framework or as proof that all repairs are impossible. Independent case classification and separate closure/stopping-layer criteria remain open. Threshold mobility retains its local definition; its qualitative relation to revisability does not establish formal equivalence with error tolerance. The stronger bidirectional claim in the Japanese source remains unresolved; it is not imported into this English draft as a proved equivalence.

The knowledge and stopping vocabulary below retains its local diagnostic use. It is not an independent criterion for the active retention roles in Section 5: observing a stop, naming its support LE, and then explaining that stop by LE would be circular. The distinction between operational stability and truth, and the unresolved mobility/error relation, are not redefined by this architectural guard.


The Gettier problem shows that justified true belief can still fail to be knowledge. In the present framework, this is not an anomaly in epistemology. It is reinterpreted using the local distinction between closure degree $C$ and truth, not as independent evidence for that distinction.

\subsection{Structural Diagnosis}

A belief can be justified, true, and yet accidentally true. Its stopping structure is stable enough to be accepted, but the path by which it arrived at truth is not structurally appropriate. The problem is therefore not simply missing justification. It is the non-identity of stability and truth.

$L_E$ names a proposed role in revisable stopping, not guaranteed truth. Logs, external reference, and correction are architecture candidates; the Gettier reinterpretation does not establish their universal necessity.

\subsection{$C$ as Stability Measure}

Closure degree $C$ measures operable stability. It does not measure truth. If $C$ were truth, then higher closure would monotonically approach knowledge. The model's reinterpretation of Gettier cases does not independently test this identification. A state can possess high closure and still fail as knowledge.

The correct reading is:
\[
C = \text{degree of operational closure/stability},
\]
not
\[
C = \text{truth}.
\]

\subsection{Error Tolerance and $\mu_\theta$}

Threshold mobility is proposed to be related to revisability and error tolerance, not established as implying them. If a system can reorganize its stopping region, it can install a stopping operator in a region that later proves inadequate. A relation between error tolerance and threshold mobility is hypothesized; the two are not established as formally equivalent or independently measured by the same criterion.

Perfect correctness, threshold immobility, and rigidity are not established as implying one another. The relation of error tolerance to $\mu_\theta$ remains a hypothesis with unresolved formal and empirical criteria.

\subsection{Consequence}

Revisable stopping supported by logs, reference, and correction is the model's proposed account of knowledge. The Gettier reinterpretation is diagnostic; it does not independently confirm this architecture or its general persistence.
