\section{Preliminaries: VED Vocabulary in the Intelligence Layer}

\noindent\textbf{Scope of the account.} Operational stability remains the local meaning of closure, not truth. The inherited evolution expression is used as a formal template; its exact provenance and adoption scope, geometry, initial/boundary data, regularity, coefficient ranges, and normalization are not fixed here. No new assumptions are adopted. A reciprocal barrier is not by itself a proof of an invariant or inaccessible boundary; ultimate understanding is a separate cognitive interpretation without an independent endpoint. The within-region/reconfiguration distinction is retained; outside-region divergence is hypothetical. Emergence notation is a schema, not a typed generative operator or an existence result. The biological subset reading also awaits a common-domain decision.

The applications of emergence vocabulary to LR and LE, and the architecture previews in this chapter, retain their proposed historical scope. For the active retained-trace and quasi-stable roles, use the scoped prose account in Section 5 rather than treating a matching schema as role evidence. This distinction does not redefine the generic VED or biological vocabulary, or any flow-field object.


This chapter fixes the vocabulary needed for the rest of the paper. The aim is not to import all of VED or IFGT, but to isolate the minimum structure required to describe inference, stopping, projection, and emergent stopping layers.

The IFGT vocabulary used here follows the projected-uses stance adopted in the consciousness paper. Symbols such as $I$, $J$, $\Phi$, and projection are not direct identifications with the full IFGT field. They are effective coordinates projected into the intelligence-layer observation window.

\subsection{Methodological Fixing: Stopping, Projection, and Consensus}

Stopping is not truth. In this paper, a stopping point is a point at which inference can be operated as provisionally stable. It may later be revised. Accordingly, the closure degree $C$ should be read as a stability measure, not as a truth measure.

Threshold mobility $\mu_\theta$ retains its local reconfiguration role. Its relation to regions where a stopping operator $E$ can be installed is a mechanism hypothesis, distinct from the definition and from an empirical criterion for sapiens-like intelligence.

Projection and consensus are read as operations of stopping structure. Attachments to ideals, gods, communities, money, scientific theories, and other loci can be structurally interpreted as installations of stopping operators. This paper does not enter psychological or clinical accounts of projection, but it keeps the structural relation between projection and stopping explicit.

The social blockchain discussed later is not blockchain as version-control technology. Its center is not Version Control but Consensus: the sharing and synchronization of stopping structures across agents.

Science and religion are conceptually reinterpreted through revision and maintenance of stopping structures. Neither an implemented social mechanism nor a general persistence or cost law follows from assigning these roles.

\subsection{VED Basic Form}

VED begins from difference. Where there is difference, there is a gradient; where there is a gradient, there is flow; where flow is constrained, quasi-closure and attractors can emerge. The following preserved schema records the historical local-state/justification reasoning in the intelligence layer:
\[
\text{difference} \;\longrightarrow\; F(L_F) \;\longrightarrow\; \text{non-closure} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{externalized stopping}.
\]

\noindent\textbf{Historical schema boundary.} The preceding arrows are not derived from the active realized-record or generative-position interfaces in Section 2. They neither establish universal external stopping nor specify the type or existence of E. The historical application to the inference layer is not an application of the new record core.

The closure degree $C$ measures how close a state is to operable stability. The information-field drive can be written schematically as a function of non-closure:
\[
I = f(1-C).
\]
As $C$ approaches the stated boundary $C_{\max}$, the reciprocal expression motivates a barrier interpretation. Inaccessibility under the full dynamics is not established.

\subsection{Movement Region and Threshold Mobility}

Let $\Omega_\theta$ denote the movement region available under a constraint parameter $\theta$. We distinguish two movement types:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(A) movement within a fixed region}: the state moves inside a given $\Omega_\theta$;
\item \textbf{(B) reconfiguration of the region}: $\theta$ itself changes, thereby changing $\Omega_\theta$.
\end{itemize}

The historical local-state model's non-closure theorem in \S{}3 applies to (A), movement within fixed $\theta$. The emergence hypothesis in \S{}5 invokes (B), region reconfiguration. This distinction is essential. The fixed-point discussion additionally depends on the stated strict-increase premise and the chosen stopping model. Region reconfiguration motivates a separate emergence hypothesis, not an established route from nonclosure to stopping.

\subsection{Emergence}

This paper uses emergence in the VED sense:
\[
U = \Phi(L,H_{ij}),
\]
where lower-layer motion $L$ and external mutual constraint $H_{ij}$ are proposed inputs in a schema for an upper-layer attractor $U$. Emergence is not a vague increase of complexity. It is the formation of an attractor from non-closed motion under mutual constraint.

For Intelligence Part II, the relevant substitution is:
\[
L_E = \Phi(L_R,H_{ij}).
\]
The emergent stopping layer $L_E$ is proposed in relation to recursive log motion $L_R$ and external mutual constraint. The schema is not a deduction of its existence or convergence.
