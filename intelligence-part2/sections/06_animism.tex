\section{Animism: Installation of Stopping in the Environment}

\noindent\textbf{Scope of the account.} Animism is treated as a conceptual reinterpretation of response attribution and stopping-site placement. That naming does not demonstrate a channel-injection mechanism. The low-cost reading is a structural hypothesis, not an explanation of universality. Historical case selection, independent social indicators, and comparative evidence remain open.

The environmental stopping-role interpretation in this chapter uses the scoped architecture convention of Section 5. Comparisons involving environmental supports or Physarum do not identify one common support or establish the recipient, continuity, or role criterion of an actual instance. Unspecified evidence remains Unknown; this note leaves the animism interpretation in its stated conceptual scope.


Animism is treated here not as an error about spirits, but as an early operating form of stopping structures. It is interpreted as assigning stopping roles to environmental loci.

In silent or under-responsive regions, inference cannot obtain sufficient return from the world. Animistic operation assigns stopping power to places, objects, winds, stones, rivers, animals, or events. Structurally, this means that an external element is made into a locus at which inference may stop, orient, or act.

This does not require the theory to assert the existence of spirits. Nor does it require the theory to dismiss animism as illusion. The question is structural: what operation does animism perform for finite intelligence? The proposed interpretation places the role of $L_E$ in the environment; this is not a demonstrated historical mechanism.

This form is close to environmental mutual constraint. The stopping locus is not yet centralized. It is local, concrete, and embedded in direct interaction between agent and environment. In this sense, animism is an installation operation:
\[
\text{environmental locus} \;\mapsto\; E\text{-installation site}.
\]

The comparison assigns stopping roles to environmental loci before considering centralization as a separate role. This ordering is conceptual, not independent evidence for historical installation or a necessary sequence.
